The Silence Between Rings

Chapter 2

The Disconnected Number

A psychological mystery / Crime thriller

Detective Jun Arata disliked being summoned before breakfast.

Especially when the person summoning him was an emergency dispatcher who claimed to have received a call from a dead woman.

He looked at Ema across the small meeting room.

“Let’s start again.”

Ema sighed.

“I’ve already explained it twice.”

“And I’m asking for a third time.”

“You’re enjoying this.”

“Not particularly.”

He opened his notebook.

“Last night, 2:17 AM. Same number. Same woman?”

“Yes.”

“She identified herself as Rina.”

“Yes.”

“Rina Shiraishi?”

Ema nodded.

Jun looked through the attached documents.

“She was last seen eleven years ago. The official investigation treated her disappearance as a probable death following the Aoba House fire.”

“Her body was never found.”

“Correct.”

“Then why was she declared dead?”

“Presumption of death after the required period. There was no confirmed body.”

Ema folded her arms.

“So she could be alive.”

“Possible. Doesn’t mean she is.”

Jun placed the documents down.

“Do you personally know her?”

“I lived at Aoba House.”

He looked up.

“For how long?”

“Seven months. I was fourteen.”

“And she worked there?”

“She was the night supervisor.”

Jun studied her for a moment.

“You didn’t mention that in the initial report.”

“Because I didn’t think it mattered.”

“It matters now.”

Ema looked away.

“She helped me when I was there.”

“Then you understand why we need to be careful. You might recognize her voice, but that doesn’t establish who’s making these calls.”

Ema nodded.

“I know.”

Jun stood.

“Show me the recording.”

They listened to both calls.

Once.

Twice.

Then again.

Jun stopped the audio after the three knocks.

“What do you think?”

Ema looked at him.

“About what?”

“The knocks.”

“Three knocks.”

“Exactly.”

He replayed them.

“Could be someone tapping a desk.”

“Or a door.”

“Or something else.”

Ema sighed.

“You detectives always have a way of making simple things sound complicated.”

Jun smiled faintly.

“It’s usually the other way around.”

He opened the technical report.

“The number wasn’t active. The caller ID was carried through an old maintenance route.”

“Meaning?”

“Someone could have used an outdated connection to display that number. The number itself doesn’t prove the caller was inside Aoba House.”

“So whoever called knows how to manipulate the system.”

“Or had access to someone who does.”

Jun closed the laptop.

“There’s another problem.”

“What?”

“Someone else has been asking about Rina Shiraishi.”

An hour later, a woman walked into the investigation office carrying a folder.

She had short dark hair, a camera hanging from her shoulder, and an expression that suggested she hadn’t slept properly in several days.

“Detective Arata?”

Jun looked up.

“Yes?”

“Miyako Shiraishi. Journalist.”

She placed a business card on the desk.

“I’m investigating the Aoba House fire.”

Ema looked at the surname.

Shiraishi.

Miyako noticed.

“You’re the dispatcher?”

Ema nodded.

Miyako’s expression changed.

“Did she call you?”

Neither detective answered immediately.

Miyako understood.

“She called.”

Ema stood.

“How do you know?”

Miyako opened her folder.

Inside was a photograph of a young woman standing outside a small building.

Rina.

The same face.

A little younger than Ema remembered.

“She’s my older sister.”

Silence.

Miyako continued.

“Eleven years ago, I was thirteen. My sister worked at Aoba House. When the fire happened, the authorities said she had probably died trying to help the residents.”

“But there was no body,” Ema said.

Miyako looked at her.

“Exactly.”

Jun pulled out a chair.

“Why are you investigating this now?”

“Because someone has been sending me letters.”

“From your sister?”

“I don’t know.”

She took out an envelope.

No return address.

Inside was a handwritten note.

THE BUILDING IS GOING TO DISAPPEAR. FIND THE ROOM BEFORE THEY DO.

Jun read it twice.

“When did you receive this?”

“Three days ago.”

Ema looked at the handwriting.

“That’s hers.”

Miyako turned toward her.

“You remember?”

“She used to write notes for residents. Same handwriting.”

Miyako looked down.

“Then she’s alive.”

Jun wasn’t convinced.

“Or someone wants you to believe she is.”

Miyako’s expression hardened.

“Detective, I’ve spent eleven years being told to stop looking for my sister. I’m done listening.”

Jun closed the folder.

“Fine. But you’ll follow our instructions.”

Miyako gave a humourless laugh.

“That’s not really my speciality.”

That evening, the three of them visited the municipal archive.

The Aoba House fire report was incomplete.

Several pages were missing.

The building was registered as having sixteen rooms.

Sixteen.

But Ema remembered seventeen doors.

She pointed at the old floor plan.

“There’s something wrong.”

Jun leaned closer.

“What?”

“There was another room.”

Miyako looked at her.

“Where?”

“Near the west corridor. Behind the staircase.”

Jun checked the diagram.

“There’s a storage area there.”

“No. It wasn’t storage.”

Ema stared at the floor plan.

“I remember a telephone.”

Miyako turned a page.

“Wait.”

An old inventory list appeared.

TELEPHONE EQUIPMENT — ONE INTERNAL LINE
LOCATION: UNLISTED

Jun looked at the entry.

“An unlisted telephone?”

Ema nodded slowly.

“Room seventeen.”

Miyako whispered:

“That’s the room.”

Jun closed the file.

“Tomorrow we’re going to Aoba House.”

He looked at the two women.

“And nobody goes there alone.”
