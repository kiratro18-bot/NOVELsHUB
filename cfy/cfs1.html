The Silence Between Rings

Chapter 1

2:17 AM

A psychological mystery / Crime thriller

2:16 AM.

Ema Kurose hated the night shift.

Not because she was afraid of the dark. She wasn’t particularly afraid of anything that could be explained.

She hated the silence.

During the day, the emergency dispatch centre was full of voices. People asking questions, operators switching lines, supervisors shouting instructions, and telephones ringing so frequently that nobody had time to think about the last call.

At night, everything slowed down.

The fluorescent lights seemed brighter. The coffee tasted worse. Even the clock sounded louder.

Ema checked the monitor.

Three active calls. One ambulance request. A traffic accident near the eastern bridge.

Nothing unusual.

She took a sip of coffee.

Cold.

“Fantastic.”

Her colleague, Fujita, glanced at her from the neighbouring desk.

“You’ve been saying that about the coffee for six months.”

“And yet they keep giving me the same coffee.”

“Maybe it’s personal.”

“Then I hope it gets over whatever I did to it.”

Fujita chuckled.

Ema returned to her screen.

2:17 AM.

A telephone rang.

One incoming call.

Ema placed her coffee down and answered.

“Emergency services. What’s your location?”

Silence.

She checked the incoming signal.

The connection was stable.

“Hello? Can you hear me?”

A faint breath.

Then a woman’s voice.

“Please...”

Ema straightened.

“Ma’am? Are you in danger?”

“Please don’t let them find the room.”

Ema’s fingers moved toward the keyboard.

“Which room? Can you tell me where you are?”

A pause.

“The room between the rings.”

Ema frowned.

“I’m sorry. I need you to speak clearly. Are you at home?”

“No.”

“Are you outside?”

“No.”

The woman’s breathing became unsteady.

“Please. They’re coming back.”

“Who’s coming?”

A faint noise.

Three soft knocks.

Then silence.

“Hello?”

The call disconnected.

Ema stared at the screen.

CALL ENDED — 00:01:43

She immediately opened the caller information.

The number appeared beneath the call duration.

She read it twice.

Then a third time.

It was a landline.

Not unusual by itself.

But the registration information was strange.

REGISTERED USER:AOBA HOUSE
SERVICE STATUS:DISCONNECTED
LAST ACTIVE:11 YEARS AGO

Ema leaned back.

“Fujita?”

“Hm?”

“Can you check something?”

He rolled his chair over.

She pointed at the number.

“Have you seen this?”

Fujita looked at the screen.

His eyebrows rose.

“That’s impossible.”

“That’s what I thought.”

“Could be a routing error.”

“Maybe.”

He checked the connection details.

“Looks like a legacy identifier. I’ll ask technical support.”

Ema opened the call transcript.

Most of it had been captured.

Except for the final three seconds.

Those were blank.

Not an interruption.

Not corrupted text.

Just silence.

She replayed the audio.

The woman’s voice sounded familiar.

She couldn’t place it.

Three knocks.

Then silence.

“Did you get the location?” Fujita asked.

“No.”

“Then document it. We can’t do much with a call like that.”

Ema nodded.

She filed the incident.

But before closing the window, she looked at the registered user again.

Aoba House.

She knew that name.

She just didn’t want to remember why.

The following night.

2:16 AM.

Ema was halfway through a report when the telephone rang.

She looked at the clock.

One minute early.

The number appeared.

The same number.

She answered immediately.

“Emergency services. What’s your location?”

This time, the woman spoke before Ema could ask anything else.

“You’re still working nights.”

Ema stopped typing.

“Who is this?”

A quiet laugh.

“Your voice hasn’t changed much.”

Ema sat upright.

“Do I know you?”

“You used to count the rings.”

Her stomach tightened.

“What?”

“One. Two. Three.”

A pause.

“Then you’d wait.”

Ema slowly looked at the caller information.

“What’s your name?”

“Rina.”

The name hit her harder than she expected.

“Rina...?”

“Tell Miyako I’m sorry.”

The connection ended.

Ema didn’t move.

She read the name in her notes.

RINA.

Then she searched the caller’s registered number again.

This time, she opened the archived user details.

An old address appeared.

Aoba House.

Former youth residence.

The registered contact was listed as:

RINA SHIRAISHI — NIGHT SUPERVISOR

Ema removed her headset.

For several seconds, she just sat there.

Fujita noticed.

“Everything alright?”

Ema shook her head.

“No.”

She looked at him.

“I think someone who’s been dead for eleven years just called us.”

The morning news played quietly on a television above the cafeteria.

A report discussed the recent execution of a criminal known publicly as Vaneiro, following the conclusion of a major investigation. The report lasted less than thirty seconds.

Nobody paid much attention.

Ema was looking at an old photograph on her phone.

Aoba House.

She had lived there for seven months when she was fourteen.

It was supposed to be temporary.

Nothing in her life had ever stayed temporary for long.

She remembered the peeling yellow walls. The terrible food. The little garden behind the building.

And Rina.

Rina Shiraishi.

She had been the night supervisor.

She wasn’t particularly strict. She used to bring books for the residents and sometimes stayed late to listen to them complain about things that nobody else thought mattered.

Ema remembered one thing clearly.

Whenever the residents couldn’t sleep, Rina would sit outside their rooms and count the rings of an old telephone.

It was an outdated ritual.

Nobody knew why she did it.

Ema had asked once.

Rina had answered:

“The silence between rings is where you hear whether someone is still there.”

Ema locked her phone.

She hadn’t thought about those words in eleven years.

And now Rina had repeated them.

But that wasn’t possible.

Because eleven years ago, Aoba House had burned.

And Rina Shiraishi had disappeared.

She had eventually been declared dead.
