Ch. 09

The Girl in the Photograph

she has been waiting for you to remember.

I didn't tell Mom about the key.

I didn't tell her about the messages either.

Mostly because I didn't know how.

"Hey, Mom. Remember that mysterious notebook you said ruined Dad's life? Someone just texted me about a girl I apparently forgot."

Yeah.

That conversation would go well.

So I put the key in my pocket and went upstairs.

I barely slept.

Again.

At some point, I started wondering if this was just what being a teenager was supposed to be like.

Homework.

Exams.

Bad sleep.

Mysterious notebooks connected to your missing father.

Maybe I was doing school wrong.

Monday morning came too quickly.

Kenta noticed immediately.

"You look dead."

"I'm fine."

"You say that like you're trying to convince me."

"I'm just tired."

"Again?"

"Yeah."

He looked at me for a few seconds.

Then he pushed a carton of milk across my desk.

"Drink."

I looked at it.

"What is this?"

"Milk."

"I know what milk is."

"Then why did you ask?"

I opened it.

"Thanks."

He shrugged.

"Don't die before the math test."

"That's touching."

"I need someone to copy homework from."

"There it is."

He smiled.

For a moment, things felt normal.

I almost wished they would stay that way.

Then Haru walked in.

He looked at me.

Then at my pocket.

I don't know how he knew.

But he did.

"Did you find something?"

I froze.

"What?"

"The key."

Kenta looked between us.

"What key?"

"Nothing," I said.

Haru kept looking at me.

"You shouldn't have it."

I lowered my voice.

"Where did you see it?"

"I didn't."

"Then how do you know?"

He didn't answer.

Kenta frowned.

"Okay, what the hell are you two talking about?"

"Nothing," Haru said.

I looked at him.

"You really like that word."

He gave me a tired smile.

"Yeah."

Then he sat down.

I waited until Kenta was distracted before taking the key out.

I placed it on my desk.

Haru stared at it.

"Where did you get that?"

"At home."

"From your father?"

I nodded.

Haru's expression changed.

"Then you need to put it back."

"No."

"Aki."

"I want answers."

"So do I."

"Then help me."

He looked away.

"I can't."

"Why?"

"Because I don't remember enough."

I wanted to argue.

Instead, I asked,

"Do you remember the girl?"

His head turned.

"What girl?"

"The one in the photograph."

He went completely still.

"You saw her?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

"At school."

He looked toward the classroom door.

"Who was she?"

"I don't know."

"Did she talk to you?"

"No."

"Then how do you know her?"

"I don't."

He stared at me.

"You're lying."

"I saw her in the photograph."

"No."

His voice was quiet.

"You remember her."

I didn't answer.

He leaned closer.

"What did she say?"

I hesitated.

"She told me not to forget her."

Haru looked down.

Then he laughed.

A short, nervous laugh.

"Of course."

"What?"

"Nothing."

I almost grabbed his shoulder.

"Stop saying that."

He looked at me.

"Her name was Yui."

The name felt familiar.

Not enough to remember.

Just enough to bother me.

"Yui what?"

"I don't know."

"You're kidding."

"I'm not."

He rubbed his eyes.

"Yui was there that day."

"April 17?"

"Yeah."

"What happened to her?"

"I don't know."

"Did she disappear too?"

"No."

"Then where is she?"

Haru looked at me.

"That's the problem."

Before I could ask what he meant, the bell rang.

Our teacher entered.

Haru turned around.

The conversation was over.

For the rest of the morning, I couldn't concentrate.

Yui.

The name kept circling around in my head.

I wrote it in the corner of my notebook.

Yui.

Then, without thinking, I wrote another word beneath it.

Rain.

I stopped.

Why had I written that?

I crossed it out.

During lunch, I went to the library.

I searched the school records again.

This time I searched for Yui.

There were several.

Yui Tanaka.

Yui Mori.

Yui Nakamura.

None of them looked familiar.

I kept searching.

Then I found a name.

Kurosawa Yui.

Class 3-B.

Last year.

Status: Transferred.

I stared at the screen.

Transferred.

Again.

I checked the date.

April 18.

The day after April 17.

I leaned back.

"Seriously?"

A voice behind me said,

"Looking for me?"

I turned.

Nobody was there.

My heart jumped.

Then I realized it had come from the computer beside me.

Kenta was sitting there.

"You scared me."

"Sorry."

"What are you doing here?"

"Same thing as you."

"I'm not doing anything."

He pointed at the screen.

"You're literally searching names."

I closed the page.

"I was curious."

"About?"

"Yui."

His expression changed.

"Oh."

"You know her?"

"Not really."

"But you remember her?"

He stared at the desk.

"I remember a girl."

"Was she in our class?"

"I think so."

"What do you remember?"

He took a breath.

"She used to sit near the window."

My stomach tightened.

"Anything else?"

"She was always drawing."

"Drawing what?"

"People."

"People?"

"Mostly."

He looked at me.

"She drew you a lot."

I didn't know what to say.

"Me?"

"Yeah."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

He shrugged.

"Maybe she liked drawing your stupid face."

I laughed.

"That's possible."

He smiled.

Then his smile disappeared.

"There was one thing."

"What?"

"She never drew your eyes."

I frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"I don't know."

He stood up.

"I've got to go."

"Wait."

He stopped.

"Why did she transfer?"

"I don't know."

"Everyone keeps transferring."

"Yeah."

"That's weird."

He nodded.

"Very."

He walked away.

I looked at the computer again.

Kurosawa Yui.

Transferred April 18.

I wrote the name down.

Then I remembered the key.

I took it out.

There was a tiny number engraved near the top.

17.

I looked around the library.

Then I understood.

Room 17?

Locker 17?

Something else?

I checked the old school map.

There were storage lockers in the basement.

Locker numbers.

17 was one of them.

I went downstairs.

The basement smelled like dust and old paper.

Most of the lockers were unused.

I found number 17.

The key fit.

I turned it.

Click.

The door opened.

Inside was a cardboard box.

Nothing else.

I pulled it out.

There was a name written on the top.

Kurosawa Yui.

I sat down on the floor.

My hands were shaking slightly.

Inside the box were drawings.

Dozens of them.

Most were ordinary.

Students.

Teachers.

The school courtyard.

The cafeteria.

But there was one drawing on top.

Me.

I recognized the uniform.

The classroom.

The window.

It had been drawn from behind.

I was sitting at the desk.

Next to me was another student.

His face was completely blacked out.

Under the drawing, Yui had written:

He hasn't noticed yet.

I turned it over.

There was another drawing.

This time, Yui had drawn herself.

She was standing beside me.

Behind us was the classroom.

And behind her—

someone else.

A tall figure.

Their face was crossed out.

I looked at the date.

April 16.

One day before everything happened.

I pulled out the next drawing.

April 17.

The classroom was empty.

Except for me.

And the notebook.

I stared at it.

Then I noticed something written at the bottom.

Not in Yui's handwriting.

It was mine.

I told her I wouldn't forget.

I dropped the paper.

For several seconds, I just sat there.

I couldn't breathe properly.

I knew that sentence.

I had heard myself say it.

Not recently.

A long time ago.

I picked up the drawing again.

There was one final note written behind it.

If Aki finds this, tell him the truth.

Then, underneath:

Yui didn't transfer.

I looked at the empty box.

There was one last envelope.

I opened it.

Inside was a photograph.

Not from April 17.

Not from last year.

It had been taken recently.

Very recently.

The picture showed the school gate.

And standing outside it was a girl.

I knew her immediately.

Yui.

Under the photograph was an address.

And one sentence.

She has been waiting for you to remember.
