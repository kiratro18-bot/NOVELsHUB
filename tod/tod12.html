Ch. 12

We Did

For a few seconds, I forgot how to speak.

My father was standing in front of me.

Not in a photograph. Not in some half-remembered dream.

Actually standing there.

He looked different.

His hair had gotten shorter, there were a few gray strands near his temples, and he looked considerably thinner than I remembered.

He was also wearing the ugliest jacket I'd ever seen.

Seriously. Where did he get that?

I had imagined meeting him again countless times.

Sometimes I imagined shouting at him.

Sometimes I imagined asking why he left.

Once, when I was younger, I even imagined running up and hugging him.

That was embarrassing to remember.

Now that he was actually here, I couldn't think of anything.

He looked at me.

"Hi, Aki."

...

"You're alive."

He nodded.

"Yeah."

"That's it?"

He tilted his head.

"What?"

"Nothing. Just... that's all you have to say?"

He looked slightly uncomfortable.

"Would you prefer a speech?"

I almost laughed.

Almost.

"You're an idiot."

"Your mother says that too."

I looked away.

That sounded like something he'd say.

And somehow, that made everything worse.

I pulled a chair away from the desk and sat down.

He remained standing for a moment before taking the seat opposite me.

Yui quietly moved toward the windows.

She seemed to understand that this conversation wasn't really hers.

I looked at my father.

"Why did you leave?"

He folded his hands.

"That's a long story."

"I have time."

He looked at the clock.

"So do I, apparently."

I stared at him.

He sighed.

"Sorry."

A few seconds passed.

Then he spoke.

"Do you remember anything about April 17?"

"Bits and pieces."

"Good."

"Good?"

"Well, not good. I mean..."

He rubbed his forehead.

"Forget it."

I leaned forward.

"Can you stop doing that?"

"Doing what?"

"Starting an explanation and then abandoning it."

He gave a small laugh.

"Fair enough."

He reached for the second notebook.

Ren's notebook.

Its cover looked almost identical to mine. Same black colour, same worn edges, even the same little scratch near the spine.

"Ren found this one in the old storage room," he said.

"I know."

"Of course you do."

"He brought it to school?"

"Yes."

"And started writing in it?"

My father nodded.

"At first, he thought it was some kind of joke. The notebook contained things that had already happened. Small things. Nothing particularly interesting."

"Like someone dropping their phone?"

He looked at me.

"Exactly."

I frowned.

"That's weird."

"It got weirder."

He opened the notebook.

"Eventually, Ren found entries written in his own handwriting. Things he didn't remember writing."

"Maybe he forgot."

"That's what he thought too."

"And?"

My father turned another page.

"Some of those entries described things that hadn't happened yet."

I looked at the notebook.

"So it predicts the future?"

"No."

He closed it.

"That's what we thought initially."

I waited.

"The notebook doesn't predict things, Aki. It records memories."

"Then why were things written before they happened?"

He didn't answer immediately.

Instead, he looked toward Yui.

She was watching us.

"That's something we never figured out."

I leaned back.

"Great. So we're still clueless."

"Pretty much."

For some reason, I laughed.

My father smiled.

It was a strange conversation to have with someone I'd thought I'd never see again.

I wanted to ask him a hundred things.

What had he been doing?

Where had he lived?

Why hadn't he called?

Did he know how old I was now?

But one question kept bothering me.

"What happened to Ren?"

My father stopped smiling.

"He's alive."

I sat straighter.

"Where?"

"I don't know."

"How can you know he's alive but not know where he is?"

"Because the last time I saw him, he was alive."

"When?"

He looked down at the notebook.

"April 17."

I swallowed.

"Then what happened?"

He didn't answer.

I glanced at Yui.

She looked away.

Something was wrong.

I could tell.

But I didn't know what.

"Can we talk about the notebook again?" I asked.

My father nodded.

He opened it to a page filled with handwriting.

"There's something you need to understand. The notebook doesn't create memories. It changes how people remember them."

"Changes?"

"Think about it. You remember something happening, but another person remembers it differently. Sometimes one person remembers something that another person has completely forgotten."

I frowned.

"That happens normally."

"Yes. But not like this."

He pointed at the pages.

"Every time we used the notebook, something changed. People forgot things. Sometimes they remembered things that weren't theirs."

I looked at Yui.

"That's why you couldn't remember April 17?"

She nodded.

"Partly."

"Partly?"

She looked uncomfortable.

"I don't remember everything, Aki."

I sighed.

"Nobody does."

"Sorry."

"No, it's fine."

I looked back at my father.

"What about me?"

He didn't answer.

"What happened to my memories?"

He took a breath.

"You were losing them faster than anyone else."

"Why?"

"Because you kept trying to remember."

I didn't understand.

He continued.

"You were obsessed with finding out what the notebook was doing. Every time something disappeared from your memory, you'd try to recover it."

"That's not a bad thing."

"No. But you weren't taking care of yourself either."

He paused.

"One day, you forgot your mother's face."

I looked at him.

"What?"

"You came to me crying. You couldn't remember what she looked like."

I didn't say anything.

My father looked at the floor.

"That was when I realised we had to stop."

I rubbed my forehead.

There was something there.

A memory.

A little boy sitting on the floor.

A woman standing in the kitchen.

A man kneeling beside him.

I couldn't see their faces clearly.

But I knew them.

My mother.

My father.

Me.

And then another memory appeared.

The classroom.

Ren sitting beside the window.

Kenta laughing at something.

Mina throwing an eraser.

Haru pretending not to listen.

Yui drawing in her notebook.

It was so ordinary.

Nobody was discussing mysteries.

Nobody was scared.

We were just students.

I suddenly missed something I couldn't properly remember.

I looked at Yui.

"Did we really spend that much time together?"

She smiled.

"Unfortunately."

"Unfortunately?"

"You were annoying."

I stared at her.

"Excuse me?"

"You kept asking what I was drawing."

"What's wrong with that?"

"You never understood my drawings."

"Maybe your drawings were bad."

She picked up a nearby eraser and threw it at me.

I caught it.

My father looked between us.

"Okay. So you two haven't changed."

For a moment, we all laughed.

It wasn't a big moment.

Nobody said anything meaningful.

But I liked it.

Then my father reached for the notebook again.

"There's something else."

I looked at him.

"What?"

"On April 17, we made a decision."

"To forget?"

He nodded.

"All of us agreed that we couldn't continue."

"Why?"

"Because we were losing too much."

He turned to the final pages.

"Ren found something here. I never got to read it properly."

"What was it?"

"I don't know."

"You're telling me you never read the last page?"

"I tried."

"And?"

He shook his head.

"Ren wouldn't let me."

I stared at him.

"Why?"

"He said it wasn't meant for me."

I looked at the notebook.

"Who was it meant for?"

My father didn't answer.

Yui stepped forward.

"Ren read it."

"Yes," my father said.

"What happened?"

He looked at her.

"He started crying."

I turned toward her.

"Why?"

She shook her head.

"I don't know."

My father continued.

"After that, he told us we had to leave. He said none of us were supposed to remember what was written there."

I looked down.

Something was bothering me.

"Then why did you write that final memory?"

My father looked at me.

"Because you asked me to."

I frowned.

"Me?"

"You wanted to remember everything. But you were forgetting people who mattered to you."

He looked toward the window.

"So I wrote that none of it had happened."

I stared at him.

"And everyone forgot?"

"More or less."

"Even you?"

He nodded.

"Even me."

I looked at the notebook.

"So why do I remember now?"

My father smiled faintly.

"I don't know."

"Seriously?"

"Seriously."

"You're supposed to be the adult here."

"Unfortunately, yes."

I shook my head.

For a moment, I thought about all the questions I still had.

The notebook.

Ren.

The old photographs.

The person who originally found it.

None of it made sense.

But there was something I wanted to know more than all of that.

"Why didn't you come home?"

My father stopped.

I looked at him.

"You knew where we lived, right?"

He nodded.

"Yes."

"Then why?"

He looked down at his hands.

"I thought staying away was the safest thing."

"For whom?"

"For you."

I laughed quietly.

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"I know."

"You know how many birthdays I had without you?"

His expression changed.

I regretted saying it.

Not because it wasn't true.

But because I hadn't planned to say it.

He looked at me for a while.

"I'm sorry."

I looked away.

There wasn't much else to say.

I wanted to be angry.

I was angry.

But I was also happy he was sitting there.

Those two feelings didn't seem to cancel each other out.

My father reached into his jacket pocket.

He took out something small.

A photograph.

He placed it on the desk.

I picked it up.

It showed our family.

My mother.

My father.

Me.

I was probably five or six.

I was holding a toy car and making a ridiculous face.

"Look at your hair," he said.

I frowned.

"What about it?"

"Terrible."

"You had that haircut."

"Mine was fashionable."

"It really wasn't."

He laughed.

I looked at the photograph again.

Then I noticed something.

On the back, there was a date.

April 17.

I turned it around.

"Why is this dated April 17?"

My father looked at it.

His smile disappeared.

"Where did you get that?"

"You just gave it to me."

He took the photograph.

For a few seconds, he studied it.

Then he shook his head.

"That's strange."

"What?"

"I don't remember writing that date."

He turned the photograph over.

There was something else written beneath it.

A short sentence.

Seven people. One promise.

I looked at him.

"What promise?"

He didn't answer.

Instead, he put the photograph back on the desk.

"Maybe we'll figure that out later."

I nodded.

I wasn't sure I wanted to.

· · ·

We left the classroom a little after eight.

The rain had stopped.

The school courtyard was wet, and the lights near the entrance were flickering.

My father locked the classroom door.

Yui was waiting near the stairs.

"Are you coming?" she asked.

"Yeah."

My father looked at her.

"Do your parents know you're out?"

She blinked.

"Yes."

"Good."

A few steps later, he asked,

"Do you two want something to eat?"

I looked at him.

"Now?"

"Yes."

"It's eight-thirty."

"People eat at eight-thirty."

"Where have you been living? Under a rock?"

He shrugged.

"Sometimes."

Yui laughed.

We walked toward the main road.

It was strangely awkward.

I didn't know what to talk about with my father.

He didn't know either.

After a while, he asked,

"How's school?"

"Fine."

"Grades?"

"Fine."

"Friends?"

"Fine."

He looked at me.

"You're not giving me much to work with."

"What do you want me to say?"

"Anything."

I thought about it.

"Kenta is an idiot."

My father nodded.

"Good. That's something."

"He's also terrible at mathematics."

"Sounds familiar."

"Hey."

He smiled.

We stopped at a small convenience store.

My father bought three sandwiches and some drinks.

Yui picked chocolate milk.

I got strawberry milk.

My father stared at the carton.

"You're still drinking that?"

"What's wrong with it?"

"Nothing."

"You were drinking it too when I was little."

"That was different."

"How?"

He opened his sandwich.

"I was young."

Yui started laughing.

I hadn't realised how much I'd missed conversations like this.

Not the dramatic ones.

Not the important ones.

Just stupid conversations about sandwiches and terrible haircuts.

For a while, I forgot about the notebook.

Then my phone vibrated.

I checked it.

No notification.

Just the screen lighting up.

I opened my notes app.

The note I'd written earlier was still there.

My father is alive.

Ren is alive.

Yui remembers.

The notebook doesn't predict the future.

And the last line:

I don't know what happens tomorrow.

I stared at it.

Then I added something.

Today, I met my father again.

I saved the note.

My father looked over.

"What are you writing?"

"Nothing."

He smiled.

"That word again."

I put my phone away.

"Shut up."

He laughed.

· · ·

We reached the bus stop.

Yui's bus arrived first.

She stood up.

"See you tomorrow."

I nodded.

"Yeah."

She hesitated.

Then looked at me.

"Aki?"

"Hm?"

"Don't force yourself to remember everything."

I didn't know what to say.

She smiled.

"Goodnight."

The bus doors closed.

I watched her disappear behind the window.

My father sat beside me.

For a while, we just waited.

Then he spoke.

"She's a good girl."

"Yeah."

"You two were close."

I looked at him.

"Really?"

He nodded.

"Very."

I looked toward the road.

"Why don't I remember that?"

He didn't answer.

A bus approached.

Not ours.

It passed.

My father leaned back.

"Do you think your mother will be angry?"

I looked at him.

"Angry?"

"About me coming back."

I thought about it.

"Probably."

He sighed.

"Great."

"She might throw something at you."

"That sounds about right."

I laughed.

Then I stopped.

"Are you coming home?"

He looked at me.

I hadn't planned to ask.

The words just came out.

He took a moment before answering.

"If she'll let me."

I nodded.

"She will."

"How do you know?"

"Because she still keeps your old coffee mug."

He looked surprised.

"She does?"

"Yeah."

He smiled.

A small one.

"That's good."

Our bus finally arrived.

We got on.

I took the window seat.

My father sat beside me.

Outside, the streets passed slowly.

People were closing shops.

A couple was arguing near a vending machine.

Some kid was running after a dog.

Ordinary things.

I rested my head against the window.

I still had questions.

A lot of them.

But I was tired.

For the first time in a long while, I didn't feel like I needed to solve everything tonight.

· · ·

When I reached home, Mom was standing in the kitchen.

She looked at me.

Then at the man behind me.

Her hand stopped moving.

Nobody spoke.

My father awkwardly raised one hand.

"Hi."

Mom stared at him.

Then she put down the cup she was holding.

"You're late."

He looked at the clock.

"Yeah."

"Food's cold."

"I ate something."

"Of course you did."

I stood between them.

Not sure whether I should leave or stay.

Then Mom looked at me.

"Go upstairs."

"Mom—"

"Please."

I nodded.

Before leaving, I looked at my father.

He gave me a small nod.

I went upstairs.

I could hear them talking.

At first, quietly.

Then louder.

I couldn't make out the words.

I sat on my bed and looked at the black notebook.

It was still inside my bag.

I took it out.

Opened it.

The pages were unchanged.

No new predictions.

No strange messages.

Just handwriting.

Mine.

Ren's.

And several others I couldn't identify.

I turned to the last page.

Blank.

I closed the notebook.

Then something slipped out.

A small photograph.

I picked it up.

It was the photograph from April 17.

The one taken in our classroom.

Seven people.

Me.

Haru.

Kenta.

Mina.

Yui.

Ren.

And my father.

I looked at the photograph for a while.

Then I noticed something.

On the back, someone had written a sentence.

I turned it over.

We promised to meet again.

I smiled faintly.

At least that sounded nice.

Then I noticed the handwriting.

It wasn't mine.

It wasn't Ren's.

It wasn't my father's.

I couldn't recognise it.

There was another line beneath it.

But one of us never came back.

I stopped smiling.

I looked at the photograph again.

Seven people.

I counted them.

Once.

Twice.

Then a third time.

There were seven.

I turned the photograph over again.

The handwriting hadn't changed.

I heard Mom calling from downstairs.

Dinner was ready.

I put the photograph inside the notebook.

Closed it.

And left it on my desk.

Tomorrow, I'd probably have more questions.

Maybe I'd ask Haru.

Maybe Kenta would remember something.

Maybe Yui would tell me what she hadn't told me yet.

And maybe, somewhere, Ren was waiting.

I switched off the light.

For now, I was home.

My father was home.

And that was enough.

At least for tonight.

The End — Volume 1.
