Ch. 07

The House

I didn't go home immediately.

I sat on the stairs for about ten minutes, staring at the same message.

Ask yourself why you don't remember going home that day.

I read it so many times that the words started looking strange.

Eventually, I deleted the message.

Then I regretted deleting it.

Then I remembered I could've taken a screenshot.

I hadn't.

"Brilliant."

I put my phone away.

The school was getting quieter.

Most students had already left.

I stood up and walked toward the front gate.

Haru was waiting there.

I stopped.

He looked at me.

"You talked to Mina."

It wasn't a question.

"Yeah."

"What did she say?"

I hesitated.

"She said Ren was at my house."

Haru looked away.

"She shouldn't have told you that."

"So it's true?"

He didn't answer.

"Haru."

"I don't know."

"You always say that."

"Because I don't know."

"You were there."

He looked at me.

"I was?"

"April 17."

His face went blank.

For a second, I thought he'd walk away.

Instead, he said,

"Come with me."

"Where?"

"Somewhere quiet."

I didn't like the sound of that.

Still, I followed him.

We walked behind the gymnasium.

There was an old bench near the storage building.

Nobody used it anymore.

Haru sat down.

I stayed standing.

He looked at the ground.

"I remember some of that day."

"Some?"

"Pieces."

"What pieces?"

He thought for a while.

"Rain."

"It rained?"

"Yeah."

I frowned.

"My photo says it was sunny."

Haru looked at me.

"What photo?"

"The one from April 17."

He went quiet.

"Show me."

I handed him my phone.

He looked at the photo.

His expression changed.

"That's not right."

"What?"

"There's someone missing."

I looked at the picture.

Me.

Haru.

Kenta.

Ren.

Mina.

Five people.

"Who?"

Haru pointed toward the corner.

"Her."

There was nobody there.

I stared at him.

"Who?"

"I don't know."

He lowered my phone.

"There was someone else."

"How do you not know?"

"Because I can't remember her."

That answer bothered me more than anything else he'd said.

I sat down beside him.

For a while, neither of us spoke.

Then I asked,

"What happened at my house?"

Haru rubbed his hands together.

"I don't remember everything."

"Just tell me what you do remember."

He took a breath.

"After school, Ren asked us to come with him."

"Why?"

"He said he wanted to show us something."

"What?"

"The notebook."

I looked at my bag.

"Then what?"

"We went to your house."

"All of us?"

"Me. You. Kenta. Mina. Ren."

I frowned.

"Mina said she wasn't with us."

"She was."

"You remember?"

"Yes."

"Then why doesn't she?"

Haru looked at me.

"Because she was the first one to forget."

I felt a chill.

"What does that mean?"

"I don't know."

He looked toward the storage building.

"Ren showed us something in your room."

"My room?"

"Yeah."

"What?"

"The notebook."

I shook my head.

"That doesn't make sense."

"I know."

"What was in it?"

"Names."

"Whose names?"

Haru looked at me.

"Ours."

I stared at him.

"All of us?"

"Yeah."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

"You keep saying that."

"Because it's true."

He stood up.

"I should go."

I grabbed his sleeve.

"Wait."

He stopped.

"What happened to Ren?"

Haru didn't move.

"You said he was at my house."

"Yes."

"Then what happened?"

He slowly turned around.

"He read the last page."

"What was on it?"

"I don't know."

"You don't remember?"

"I remember him reading it."

"And?"

"He started laughing."

"Laughing?"

"Yeah."

"Why?"

Haru looked genuinely confused.

"I don't know."

He pulled his sleeve free.

"Then he said something."

"What?"

Haru stared at me.

"He said, 'So that's why I forgot.'"

I felt my stomach drop.

"What did he forget?"

Haru shook his head.

"I don't know."

He walked away.

I didn't follow him.

I went home alone.

My mother was in the kitchen.

She looked at me.

"You're late."

"Yeah."

"Everything okay?"

"Yeah."

She went back to cooking.

I stood in the doorway.

Then I asked,

"Mom?"

"Hm?"

"Did Ren ever come here?"

She stopped.

Only for a second.

Then she continued cutting vegetables.

"Who?"

"Shinoda Ren."

She looked at me.

"I don't know anyone by that name."

I stared at her.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

She smiled.

"Why?"

"Nothing."

There was that word again.

I went upstairs.

My room looked exactly the same as it always did.

Bed.

Desk.

Books.

Closet.

Nothing unusual.

I sat down.

Then I remembered something.

The photo.

I opened my phone and looked at it again.

Five people.

But Haru said there was someone else.

I zoomed in.

The corner near the door was dark.

I increased the brightness.

There was a shape.

A person.

I hadn't noticed them before.

They were standing behind Mina.

I zoomed in again.

The image became blurry.

I couldn't see their face.

But I could see their hand.

They were holding something.

A notebook.

I stopped.

I opened my bag.

The black notebook was still there.

I placed it beside me.

Then I looked at the photograph again.

The notebook in the photograph looked exactly the same.

Same black cover.

Same worn corner.

Same small scratch near the spine.

I opened mine.

I turned to the last page.

It was blank.

I flipped it over.

Blank.

I checked the back cover.

Nothing.

Then I noticed something.

A small piece of paper had been tucked inside.

I pulled it out.

It was old.

Yellow around the edges.

There was handwriting on it.

My handwriting.

I knew it immediately.

The note said:

If you're reading this, don't trust the notebook.

I stared at it.

I turned the paper over.

There was another sentence.

It doesn't predict anything.

I felt relieved.

For about two seconds.

Then I read the final line.

It remembers.

I looked at the notebook.

For the first time, I understood why the predictions had felt so strange.

Maybe they weren't predictions.

Maybe they were memories.

Memories that hadn't happened yet.

I sat there for a long time.

Then someone knocked on my bedroom door.

"Are you awake?"

My mother's voice.

I quickly put the note away.

"Yeah."

She opened the door.

She looked at me.

Then at the notebook.

Her expression changed.

"Where did you get that?"

I couldn't answer.

She stepped into the room.

"Give it to me."

"Why?"

"Just give it to me."

I'd never heard her sound like that before.

I held the notebook tighter.

She stared at it.

Then whispered,

"That should've been gone."

I looked at her.

"What?"

She closed her eyes.

For a moment, she looked tired.

Really tired.

Then she said something that made absolutely no sense.

"Your father found that notebook first."

I froze.

"My father?"

She nodded.

"And he disappeared because of it."
