Ch. 10

Yui

he is still writing.

The address was written on the back of the photograph.

I stared at it for a while.

It wasn't far from school.

Maybe twenty minutes by bus.

I checked the time.

4:12 p.m.

I could go.

I could also do the sensible thing and go home.

I chose the less sensible thing.

Obviously.

I put the photograph in my bag and left the school.

The bus was crowded.

I stood for most of the ride, holding onto the metal pole while two middle-school students argued about some mobile game behind me.

I tried not to think about what I was doing.

It didn't work.

Who was Yui?

Why did she know me?

Why did she disappear?

And why did everyone keep acting like they remembered something I couldn't?

The bus finally stopped near a small shopping street.

I got off.

The address led me down a quiet road lined with houses.

I checked the number again.

Then I stopped in front of a small house with a blue mailbox.

I stared at the door.

For some reason, knocking felt more difficult than it should have.

I raised my hand.

Knocked.

Nothing.

I knocked again.

Footsteps.

The door opened.

A girl stood there.

Short hair.

School uniform.

She looked slightly older than she had in the photograph.

But it was definitely her.

Yui.

She stared at me.

For a moment, neither of us spoke.

Then she smiled.

Not happily.

More like she'd been waiting for something and had finally gotten tired of waiting.

"You took long enough."

I didn't know what to say.

"You're Yui."

"Yeah."

"Kurosawa Yui?"

"Do you know another Yui?"

"No."

"Then probably."

She stepped aside.

"Are you coming in?"

I hesitated.

"Is that okay?"

She looked at me.

"You came all the way here."

"Fair."

I stepped inside.

The house was quiet.

There were books everywhere.

Not neatly arranged.

Just piles of them.

A cup sat on the table with a tea bag still floating inside.

Yui pointed toward the sofa.

"Sit."

I sat.

She sat across from me.

For a few seconds, she just looked at me.

I started feeling uncomfortable.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You've said that too."

She smiled.

"You really haven't changed."

That sentence bothered me.

"Have we met?"

"Yes."

"When?"

She looked down.

"That's what we're trying to figure out, right?"

I didn't understand.

She stood up.

"Do you want tea?"

"No."

"Good."

She walked into the kitchen.

I stared at her.

"Then why did you ask?"

"I don't know."

She came back without anything.

She sat down again.

"Show me."

"What?"

"The photograph."

I took it out.

She looked at it.

Her expression didn't change.

"You found the box."

"Yeah."

"Where?"

"Basement."

She nodded.

"I thought they'd thrown it away."

"Who?"

"The school."

"Why were your drawings there?"

"Because I left them there."

"Why?"

She shrugged.

"I was told to."

"By who?"

She didn't answer.

I leaned forward.

"Yui."

She looked at me.

"Do you remember April 17?"

"Yes."

"Everything?"

"No."

That surprised me.

"You don't?"

"No."

She smiled slightly.

"Nobody remembers everything."

"Then what do you remember?"

She looked toward the window.

"Rain."

I frowned.

"Again?"

"What?"

"Haru said it rained."

"It did."

"But my photograph says it was sunny."

Yui laughed.

"The photograph was taken before it rained."

"When?"

"4:26."

I looked at her.

"How do you know?"

"I took it."

My stomach dropped.

"You took that photograph?"

"Yeah."

"Why?"

She looked at me.

"Because you asked me to."

I couldn't speak.

"You asked me to take a picture."

"Why?"

"You said you wanted proof."

"Proof of what?"

She shook her head.

"I don't remember."

I sat back.

"So what happened after the photo?"

"Do you really want to know?"

"Yes."

She looked at me for a long time.

Then she said,

"Ren came to your house."

I already knew that.

But hearing it from her made it feel real.

"What happened there?"

"We found your father."

I froze.

"My father?"

"Yes."

"That's impossible."

"Why?"

"Because my father disappeared years ago."

Yui frowned.

"Years?"

"Yeah."

She looked genuinely confused.

"How long?"

"I don't know. A long time."

She leaned back.

"That's not what happened."

"What do you mean?"

"Your father was there."

"When?"

"April 17."

"Last year?"

She nodded.

I stared at her.

"That doesn't make sense."

"I know."

"Was he alive?"

"Yes."

My hands went cold.

"Where is he now?"

"I don't know."

"Yui."

"I'm serious."

She looked frustrated.

"That's all I remember."

I stood up.

"Then why did Mom say he disappeared?"

"I don't know."

"Why does everyone know something except me?"

Yui stood too.

"Because you asked us to forget."

I stopped.

"What?"

"You asked us."

"Me?"

"Yes."

"Why would I do that?"

"Because you were scared."

I shook my head.

"I don't remember."

"I know."

She walked toward a shelf.

Pulled out a small box.

Inside were photographs.

She handed me one.

It showed the classroom.

April 17.

I looked at the people.

Me.

Haru.

Kenta.

Mina.

Ren.

Yui.

And my father.

Seven people.

I stared at him.

He was standing beside the window.

He looked directly at the camera.

At me.

I turned the photograph over.

There was writing.

We agreed not to remember.

I looked at Yui.

"Who agreed?"

"All of us."

"Why?"

"Because of the notebook."

I pulled the black notebook from my bag.

Yui immediately stepped back.

"Don't open it."

"Why?"

"Because it isn't the same anymore."

"What does that mean?"

She shook her head.

"I don't know."

"You said you remember."

"Pieces."

She pointed at the notebook.

"That thing makes people remember things differently."

"How?"

"Sometimes it writes things before they happen."

"Predictions."

"No."

She looked at me.

"It writes things after they happen."

I frowned.

"That's what Haru said."

"Then why are you still calling them predictions?"

I didn't answer.

Yui sat down.

"Think about it."

She pointed at the notebook.

"Tuesday. Someone dropped their phone."

"Yes."

"Wednesday. Someone forgot their umbrella."

"Yes."

"Thursday. Someone asked about the notebook."

"Yes."

"Friday. Someone asked what happened to Ren."

I nodded.

"Those weren't predictions."

She looked at me.

"They were memories."

I stared at the notebook.

"Memories from when?"

Yui's voice became quieter.

"I don't know."

"Then what does it want?"

She smiled sadly.

"Maybe nothing."

"What?"

"Maybe we're the ones who keep trying to make it mean something."

I didn't like that answer.

It felt too simple.

Then the notebook moved.

I dropped it.

It landed on the floor.

The pages opened by themselves.

I stared.

A blank page.

Then writing.

Slowly.

One line appeared.

Monday.

Yui went pale.

"No."

Another line appeared.

Someone will remember the truth.

I looked at Yui.

"What does that mean?"

She didn't answer.

The writing continued.

A second sentence appeared.

Aki will remember who opened the notebook.

Yui stepped backward.

"Aki..."

"What?"

She looked terrified.

"I didn't open it."

"I know."

"No."

She shook her head.

"I mean..."

She stared at the page.

"I didn't open it the first time."

My heart sank.

"Who did?"

Yui looked at me.

Then toward the window.

"The person who wrote it."

"Who?"

She whispered,

"Your father."

I stared at her.

The notebook lay open between us.

Then one final line appeared beneath the others.

He is still writing.
