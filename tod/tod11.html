Ch. 11

The Person Who Wrote It

ren didn't disappear.

For a while, neither of us moved.

The notebook was still open on the floor.

He is still writing.

I read it again.

Then again.

"Your father is alive?"

Yui didn't answer.

"Yui."

"I don't know."

I laughed once.

It came out wrong.

"Of course."

She looked at me.

"I told you. I don't remember everything."

"Then what do you remember?"

"Enough to know we shouldn't be here."

"That's not an answer."

"I know."

She picked up the notebook.

I reached for it.

She pulled it away.

"Don't."

"Give it to me."

"No."

"Yui."

"You're doing exactly what you did last time."

I stopped.

"What did I do?"

She looked at me.

"You kept asking."

"About what?"

"Everything."

She sat down on the floor.

I sat opposite her.

The notebook stayed between us.

"Tell me about April 17."

She sighed.

"You really won't stop."

"No."

"Fine."

She opened the notebook.

"Last year, Ren found this in the old storage room."

"The same notebook?"

"Yes."

"He brought it to class."

"And?"

"He started writing in it."

"Predictions?"

"No."

She shook her head.

"He wrote things that had already happened."

"Like what?"

"Small things at first."

She turned a page.

"Someone would drop a pen."

"Someone would forget homework."

"Someone would leave school early."

I looked at her.

"That's exactly what happened."

"Yes."

"But how?"

"We didn't know."

"What did Ren think?"

"He thought someone was playing a joke."

"And then?"

"He found his own handwriting."

I frowned.

"What?"

"On pages he'd never written."

She ran her finger along the paper.

"He recognized the handwriting, but he couldn't remember writing it."

"Did he get scared?"

"Eventually."

"Why?"

"Because the notebook started writing things he hadn't done yet."

I stared at her.

"So it did predict the future."

"That's what we thought."

"We?"

"Me, Ren, Haru, Kenta..."

She paused.

"Mina."

"What about me?"

Yui looked at me.

"You were there."

"I don't remember."

"I know."

She turned another page.

"On April 17, Ren wanted to test it."

"How?"

"He wrote a sentence."

"What sentence?"

She looked at me.

"Something about you."

My stomach tightened.

"What?"

"I don't remember the exact words."

"Then what do you remember?"

"That you got angry."

"At Ren?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because he told you what the notebook had written."

"What did it say?"

Yui looked down.

"It said you would forget us."

I didn't speak.

She continued.

"You thought it was a joke."

"Was it?"

"No."

"What happened?"

"You took the notebook."

"Why?"

"You said you wanted to destroy it."

I looked at the cover.

"Did I?"

"You tried."

"What happened?"

Yui shook her head.

"You couldn't."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

She paused.

"Then your father came."

I stared at her.

"My father."

"Yes."

"What did he do?"

"He already knew about the notebook."

"How?"

"Because he had found it years before."

I thought about the photographs in the album.

The old man beside him.

The boy.

"Who was the other man?"

Yui looked at me.

"I don't know."

"You're sure?"

"No."

That answer was becoming familiar.

She continued.

"Your father told us something."

"What?"

"He said the notebook wasn't predicting anything."

I waited.

"He said it was recording."

"Recording what?"

"Memory."

I frowned.

"Whose memory?"

"Everyone's."

I looked at the notebook.

"That's impossible."

"I know."

"Then why believe him?"

"Because he showed us."

"How?"

Yui looked at the page.

"He wrote something."

"What?"

"A memory."

She closed her eyes.

"And then we remembered it."

"All of us?"

"Yes."

"What memory?"

She opened her eyes.

"The first time we met."

I felt something shift inside my head.

A classroom.

A sunny afternoon.

Someone laughing.

A girl drawing.

A boy leaning over my desk.

Ren.

Then—

nothing.

I grabbed my head.

Yui stood.

"Aki?"

"I'm fine."

"You aren't."

"I said I'm fine."

My voice came out sharper than I intended.

She stepped back.

"Sorry."

I rubbed my forehead.

The memory was gone.

Completely.

I hated that.

"What happened after?"

Yui sat down again.

"Your father told us we couldn't keep using the notebook."

"Why?"

"Because every time it recorded a memory, someone lost another one."

I stared at her.

"What?"

"That's what happened to you."

I didn't answer.

"Every memory the notebook kept..."

She looked at me.

"...it took something else."

"From me?"

"From everyone."

"Then why did you use it?"

"We didn't know."

"Until when?"

"Until April 17."

I looked at the clock.

It was getting late.

"That's when you decided to forget."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because Ren found something."

"What?"

"The last page."

I remembered the photograph.

The one from April 17.

My father.

Ren.

Everyone.

"What's on the last page?"

Yui looked toward the notebook.

"I don't know."

"You said Ren read it."

"I saw him open it."

"And?"

"He started crying."

I wasn't expecting that.

"Ren cried?"

"Yeah."

"What did he say?"

Yui looked down.

"He said, 'We're not supposed to remember this.'"

"What did he mean?"

"I don't know."

"Then what?"

"Your father told us to leave."

"Why?"

"He said the notebook had already taken too much."

"From whom?"

"From you."

I felt a strange pressure in my chest.

"Me?"

Yui nodded.

"You had forgotten almost everything."

I looked at my hands.

"Then why don't you remember?"

"Because we all agreed to forget."

"How?"

"Your father knew how."

"What did he do?"

Yui hesitated.

"He used the notebook."

I stared at her.

"He wrote one final memory."

"What memory?"

She looked directly at me.

"That none of this ever happened."

The room went quiet.

I looked at the notebook.

"So that's why I forgot."

"Yes."

"But then why am I remembering now?"

Yui didn't answer.

I already knew.

"The notebook."

She nodded.

"Every time you read it, it gives something back."

"And takes something else."

"Yes."

I stood up.

"Then I should stop."

Yui nodded.

"Yes."

I put the notebook into my bag.

Then something occurred to me.

"If my father wanted us to forget, why is he still writing?"

Yui looked at me.

"I don't know."

I pulled out my phone.

There was one new message.

Unknown number.

You finally remembered.

I showed Yui.

She stared at it.

Another message arrived.

Now come back to the classroom.

I felt my stomach drop.

"Why?"

Yui looked toward the door.

"Because that's where it started."

"And?"

She didn't answer.

I waited.

Finally, she said,

"That's where your father is."

I stared at her.

"You said you didn't know where he was."

"I didn't."

"Then how do you know?"

She looked at the notebook.

"The notebook told me."

I took it out again.

The cover felt strangely warm.

I opened it.

A new page.

No date.

No prediction.

Just an address.

My school.

Room 3-B.

Underneath:

8:17 p.m.

I checked the time.

8:03.

I looked at Yui.

"We have fourteen minutes."

She nodded.

"Then let's go."

We left the house together.

Neither of us spoke on the way.

For once, I didn't have any questions.

I think I was afraid of the answers.

When we reached the school, the gates were locked.

Yui pulled a key from her pocket.

I stared at it.

"You had that the whole time?"

"Yeah."

"You could've mentioned it."

"You didn't ask."

"Seriously?"

She smiled.

"Sorry."

We slipped through the gate.

The school was completely dark.

We walked toward the classroom.

3-B.

The door was unlocked.

I pushed it open.

The room was empty.

Except for one desk.

Mine.

The seat by the window.

A black notebook sat on top of it.

Another one.

Identical to the one in my hand.

I stared at both.

Then someone spoke from behind us.

"You came."

I turned.

A man stood in the doorway.

Older.

Tired.

Familiar.

I had seen his face in photographs.

My hands went cold.

"Dad?"

He smiled.

Not happily.

Just enough.

"Hi, Aki."

I couldn't move.

Yui stepped forward.

"You said you wouldn't come back."

He looked at her.

"I said I wouldn't come back until he remembered."

Then he looked at me.

"Looks like I was late."

I stared at him.

"Why did you leave?"

He didn't answer immediately.

Instead, he looked at the two notebooks.

Then he said,

"Because one of them isn't mine."

I frowned.

"What?"

He pointed at the notebook in my hand.

"That one belongs to you."

Then he pointed at the other.

"That one belongs to Ren."

I felt my stomach drop.

"But Ren disappeared."

My father looked at me.

"No."

He shook his head.

"Ren didn't disappear."

He took a breath.

"We did."

And behind him, the classroom door slowly closed.
