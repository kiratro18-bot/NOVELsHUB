Ch. 08

My Father's Room

For a few seconds, I honestly thought I had heard her wrong.

"My father?"

Mom didn't answer.

She was still looking at the notebook.

Not at me.

At the notebook.

"Mom."

She finally looked up.

"Where did you get that?"

"School."

"School?"

"It was under my desk."

She went quiet.

Then she sat on the edge of my bed.

I had never seen her look like that.

Not scared exactly.

More like she'd just remembered something she had spent years trying not to.

"What happened to Dad?"

She looked at the floor.

"You know what happened."

"I know what everyone told me."

"Then—"

"They told me he left."

She didn't respond.

I waited.

"That's what you told me."

"I know."

"So why are you saying he disappeared?"

She rubbed her hands together.

"I shouldn't have said that."

"Mom."

"I was angry."

"At what?"

"At the notebook."

I looked down at it.

"Why?"

She stood up.

"Put it away."

"No."

"Aki."

"I want to know."

"You're too young."

"I'm not five."

"I know."

"Then tell me."

She looked at me for a long time.

Finally, she sighed.

"Your father found it in the old school building."

I frowned.

"He went to my school?"

"Before you were born."

That caught me off guard.

"What?"

"He worked there for a while."

"My father worked at my school?"

"Not as a teacher."

She paused.

"He was helping with the building renovation."

I sat down.

This was getting more confusing by the second.

"So he found the notebook there?"

"Yes."

"What did he do with it?"

"He brought it home."

"Then what?"

"He started writing in it."

"Predictions?"

Mom shook her head.

"At first, no."

She looked toward my desk.

"He wrote things that had happened."

"Like what?"

"I don't remember."

"You're lying."

She looked at me.

I regretted saying it immediately.

But she didn't get angry.

Instead, she smiled sadly.

"Maybe."

That somehow made me feel worse.

"Why did he disappear?"

Mom walked toward the door.

"I don't know."

"You just said—"

"I said I don't know."

She opened the door.

"Go to sleep."

"Mom."

She stopped.

"Did Dad know Ren?"

Her hand tightened around the door handle.

She didn't turn around.

"Who told you that name?"

"Haru."

A long pause.

Then:

"Stay away from him."

"Haru?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because people who remember too much usually end up forgetting something important."

She left.

I sat there alone.

I didn't sleep.

Not really.

I lay in bed staring at the ceiling until my eyes hurt.

At 2:00 a.m., I got up.

I went downstairs.

The house was dark.

Mom's bedroom door was closed.

I walked quietly into the living room.

There was an old cabinet beside the television.

I had never paid much attention to it.

There were old photo albums inside.

I took one out.

Dust covered the top.

I opened it.

Wedding photos.

Family trips.

My first birthday.

Dad holding me.

I stopped.

I hadn't looked at these pictures in years.

He looked younger than I remembered.

Of course he did.

I kept turning pages.

Then I found a picture of him standing in front of a building.

My school.

I recognized the windows immediately.

On the back was a date.

April 17.

I stared at it.

Last year?

No.

The picture was older.

Much older.

There was another date written underneath.

2009.

I looked at the photograph again.

Dad was standing beside someone.

An older man.

I didn't recognize him.

But he was holding the same black notebook.

I turned the page.

Another photograph.

Dad.

The same man.

And a boy.

I froze.

The boy was young.

Maybe ten or eleven.

I couldn't see his face properly.

But there was something familiar about him.

I turned the photograph over.

Nothing.

I looked through the album again.

The boy appeared in three more pictures.

Always beside my father.

Always with that same notebook somewhere nearby.

Then I found a photograph of the school classroom.

My classroom.

The desk by the window.

There was writing on the back.

Room 3-B.

And beneath it:

He remembers.

I heard a floorboard creak upstairs.

I closed the album.

Someone was awake.

I put everything back.

Then I noticed something on the floor.

A small envelope.

It hadn't been there before.

At least, I didn't think it had.

I picked it up.

My name was written on it.

Aki.

Again.

I opened it.

Inside was a key.

A small silver key.

And a note.

Your father kept the rest in the school.

I stared at it.

Then another line.

Don't let Haru find it first.

I folded the note.

My phone buzzed.

I nearly dropped it.

Unknown number.

You're looking in the wrong place.

I typed:

Who are you?

A reply came almost immediately.

Someone who remembers April 17.

I stared at the screen.

Then another message appeared.

Ask your mother what happened to the girl.

I didn't know what girl they meant.

Then I remembered the photograph.

The girl standing behind Mina.

The one Haru couldn't remember.

I opened the photo on my phone.

Zoomed in.

The girl was holding something.

Not the notebook.

A key.

The same shape as the one in my hand.

I looked at the silver key.

Then at the photograph.

My phone buzzed again.

This time, the message was only three words.

She remembers you.

I didn't know who "she" was.

But somewhere in the back of my mind, a memory surfaced.

A girl standing beside me.

Rain outside.

A classroom after everyone had left.

And her saying:

"Don't forget me, okay?"

I couldn't remember her face.

But I remembered my answer.

"I won't."

I sat on the floor.

The key was still in my hand.

And suddenly, I wasn't sure which part scared me more.

That I had forgotten her.

Or that someone else knew I had.
