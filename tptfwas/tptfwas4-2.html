The Petal That Falls With A Smile — Volume IV

Fireworks

POV: Noa

The fireworks were about to start.

I could feel it — that strange quiet that comes right before the sky explodes with light.

Then—

Boom.

The first firework bloomed open, painting the night in gold and blue.

So beautiful.

Haruki and I sat close, surrounded by people, yet it felt like just the two of us. Everyone was looking up. No one was looking at me.

This was my chance.

My heart was loud.

Say it. Just say it.

“I– I…”

The words caught in my throat.

What if he rejects me?

What if he starts avoiding me?

What if… he still loves Sora?

So many what ifs crushed the courage I had been collecting all day.

The fireworks kept going.

Red.

White.

Gold.

Each one felt like time slipping away.

And then—

Silence.

The show ended.

I hadn’t said anything.

I smiled anyway.

Because that’s what I always do.

“Hey,” Haruki said, turning to me. “You said something during the fireworks.”?

“Oh—” I laughed softly. “It was nothing.”

A lie.

The truth was simple and heavy:

I was scared.

POV: Ren

“So,” I said, trying to sound normal, “the fireworks were beautiful, right?”

Sora nodded. “Yeah… they were cool.”

But she wasn’t really there.

“Let’s grab some snacks,” I suggested. “The stalls are still open.”

She hesitated.

“No, Ren. I think I’ll head home.”

I frowned. “You okay?”

“Yeah,” she replied quickly. “Just a headache.”

She smiled — that polite smile people use when they don’t want questions.

“Alright,” I said. “Take care.”

As she walked away, something in my chest tightened.

She’s moving… but not toward me.

POV: Sora

Ren cares about me.

I know that.

He shows it in small ways — waiting, asking, noticing when I go quiet.

And yet—

Why does my heart feel so messy?

I told myself I moved on from Haruki.

I told myself I had no right to feel anything after running away like I did.

So why did seeing him tonight still hurt?

And worse…

Why am I starting to fall for Ren again?

After everything I did.

After all the confusion I caused.

This isn’t fair, I thought.

Not to him.

Not to anyone.

I walked faster, the festival lights fading behind me.

Maybe moving on isn’t about choosing someone new.

Maybe it’s about finally facing what you broke.

Narrator

That night, fireworks lit the sky—

But not every feeling reached the surface.

One girl swallowed her confession.

One boy sensed distance he couldn’t name.

And one girl realized that moving on doesn’t erase guilt.

Some chances don’t disappear loudly.

They fade.

Quietly.

End of Chapter
