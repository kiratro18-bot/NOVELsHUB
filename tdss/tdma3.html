THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 3 — Four Men, One Dead Man

Shun was not supposed to access police records outside his training.

He knew that.

He also knew Toma would probably do something stupid without him.

So, naturally, he went to the academy library.

He spent almost three hours examining public case summaries and archived news reports.

When he returned, Toma was eating instant noodles.

“You’re alive,” Toma said.

“Disappointed?”

“Just checking.”

Shun threw a folder at him.

“Read.”

Toma opened it.

Four cases.

Case One — October 2.

A break-in at the municipal records office. Several Northbridge construction documents disappeared.

Case Two — October 11.

A retired construction foreman was assaulted outside his apartment. He survived but refused to speak to investigators.

Case Three — October 18.

The disappearance of Masaki Kuroda.

Case Four — October 29.

An attempted fire at an old Northbridge storage depot.

Toma read the reports.

“Where’s my father?”

Shun pointed to the witness statements.

“Every single case contains a reference to Kaito Kisaragi.”

Toma leaned forward.

“Four times?”

“Four times.”

“That’s ridiculous.”

“I know.”

Shun opened a page.

The witness statement from Case One read:

I was with Mr Kisaragi between 8:00 PM and 9:30 PM.

The second:

Mr Kisaragi personally instructed me not to interfere.

The third:

I met Kaito Kisaragi on the evening of October 18.

The fourth:

Mr Kisaragi was present when the materials were transferred.

Toma stared.

“These sound rehearsed.”

“That’s what I noticed.”

“Same wording?”

“Almost.”

Shun took out four printed statements.

He placed them side by side.

Each contained the phrase:

I HAVE NO REASON TO DOUBT MR KISARAGI’S IDENTITY.

Toma frowned.

“Who writes like that?”

“People who want to sound convincing.”

“Or people who’ve been told what to write.”

Shun nodded.

“Exactly.”

The next afternoon, they visited the retired foreman.

His name was Hajime Ono.

He lived alone in a small apartment near the eastern railway line.

When Toma introduced himself, the old man immediately closed the door.

Shun placed his foot against it.

“Please. We’re not police.”

“That makes it worse.”

“We only want to ask about Northbridge.”

Ono’s expression changed.

He slowly opened the door.

“Come in.”

His apartment smelled of old books and green tea.

He sat opposite them.

“You’re Kaito’s boy?”

Toma nodded.

Ono looked at him for a long time.

“You have his eyes.”

Toma didn’t know what to say.

“Did you know my father?”

“Worked with him for four years.”

“Did he steal something?”

Ono frowned.

“What?”

“Four people claim he met them after his death. They’re using his name in criminal cases.”

The old man laughed.

Then stopped.

“Of course they are.”

Toma leaned forward.

“What does that mean?”

Ono stood.

He walked towards a cupboard and removed an old envelope.

“I should’ve spoken years ago.”

Inside were several documents.

A construction inspection report.

A handwritten note.

And a company payment record.

“Your father discovered that Northbridge had changed the structural materials after approval. He refused to sign the inspection.”

“Why didn’t he report them?”

“He did.”

“Then?”

Ono sat down.

“The report disappeared.”

He looked towards the window.

“Your father started collecting copies. The company found out.”

“Who?”

Ono pointed to the company payment record.

At the bottom was a signature.

MASATO GENDA — DIRECTOR, NORTHBRIDGE LOGISTICS.

Toma recognised the name.

It appeared on the company’s current redevelopment announcement.

“Why is he still running the company?”

Ono smiled bitterly.

“Because people like him rarely lose their jobs.”

Shun looked at the old man.

“Who attacked you?”

Ono didn’t answer.

Instead, he handed Toma the envelope.

“Your father told me to give this to you if he ever returned.”

Toma took it.

“Returned?”

Ono looked directly at him.

“Yes.”

Then he said something neither of them expected.

“Your father never died in that accident.”
