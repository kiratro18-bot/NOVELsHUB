THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 10 — The Last Person to Leave

The eastern archive was beneath the old terminal.

The original documents were still there.

Boxes of financial records.

Inspection reports.

Signed orders.

And a small metal cabinet containing the original construction samples.

Everything matched.

The replacement materials had been approved by Northbridge after the original inspection.

The false reports had been signed.

The payments had been recorded.

The evidence was complete.

But Masaki Kuroda was still missing.

Natsumi stood near the archive entrance.

She held the old photograph of her parents.

“Do you think he’s alive?”

Kaito looked at her.

“I believe he is.”

“How?”

“Because I know him.”

A police officer approached.

“Detective Nakamura wants to see you.”

They followed him outside.

A man sat inside the police vehicle.

His hair had grown longer.

His face was tired.

But Natsumi recognised him immediately.

“Dad?”

Masaki looked up.

For a moment, he didn’t move.

Then he stepped out.

Natsumi ran towards him.

She reached him and stopped.

There were too many questions.

Too many months.

Too much anger.

Masaki opened his arms.

“I’m sorry.”

Natsumi hit his chest once.

Not hard.

“You’re an idiot.”

He laughed through tears.

“Yes.”

“You left me.”

“I know.”

“You didn’t even leave me a proper message.”

“I know.”

She hugged him.

Toma looked away.

Mio stood beside him.

“You’re crying,” she whispered.

“No.”

“Your eyes are red.”

“Allergies.”

“To what?”

“People.”

She laughed.

For once, he didn’t argue.

The investigation continued for months.

Ueda was arrested.

Genda’s company was suspended pending prosecution.

The four cases were formally linked.

Several employees cooperated with investigators.

The original inspection reports were released to the public.

Northbridge Terminal was no longer scheduled for immediate demolition.

The press had a wonderful time with the story.

THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI.

That was the headline one newspaper used.

Toma hated it.

Shun loved it.

He had even bought a copy.

“Look at this,” Shun said, holding up the newspaper. “We’re famous.”

“We?”

“I was involved.”

“You ate noodles and complained.”

“Emotional support.”

Mio took the newspaper from him.

“Your name isn’t even mentioned.”

Shun stared.

“...That’s disappointing.”

Toma laughed.

Three months later, Toma met his father again.

This time, it wasn’t at an abandoned terminal.

It was at a small restaurant near the river.

Kaito had agreed to meet his mother.

Toma wasn’t sure how it would go.

He arrived early.

His mother was already there.

She looked nervous.

“Is he coming?”

Toma nodded.

“Yes.”

She looked down at her tea.

“Do you think he looks different?”

Toma thought about it.

“A little.”

“Older?”

“Definitely.”

She gave a small laugh.

Then the restaurant door opened.

Kaito entered.

He stopped when he saw her.

Neither moved.

Toma quietly stood.

“I’ll wait outside.”

His mother nodded.

He walked out.

For the first time in six years, his parents were speaking without him standing between them.

He hoped they would figure it out.

He wasn’t going to make that decision for them.

Outside, his phone vibrated.

A message from Mio.

Mio: Are you free?

Toma: No.

Mio: What are you doing?

Toma: Being busy.

Mio: With what?

Toma: Existing.

Mio: You’re annoying.

Toma: Thank you.

A second message arrived.

Mio: Café Lumière. Twenty minutes.

Toma smiled.

He put his phone away.

Then looked back through the restaurant window.

His father was still sitting across from his mother.

For now, that was enough.
