THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Epilogue — Nobody’s Alibi

One Year Later.

The old Northbridge Terminal was still standing.

Its ownership had changed.

Its records had been released.

And the company that once controlled the entire redevelopment project had been broken apart.

A small memorial stood near the entrance.

Six names.

Six workers who had been injured during the original incident.

Their families had finally received the truth.

“You’re late.”

Toma looked at Mio.

She was standing outside a bookstore, holding two drinks.

“Three minutes.”

“Four.”

“You’re counting?”

“Obviously.”

He took the coffee.

“Thank you.”

“You’re welcome.”

They walked down the street.

Mio was now working for a local newspaper.

She had published several investigative articles, although her editor had apparently banned her from investigating anything involving missing people without approval.

Toma had started working part-time at an architectural firm.

He was still undecided about whether he wanted to follow his father’s profession.

Shun had completed his academy training and was preparing for his first full assignment.

He remained an idiot.

Some things never changed.

They passed a small bookstore.

Inside, Ren was arguing with the owner.

Sayo stood beside him, holding a stack of books.

Toma stopped.

“What’s happening?”

Mio looked through the window.

“Looks like he’s buying something.”

Ren held up a book.

Sayo shook her head.

He held up another.

She shook her head again.

Finally, he pointed at a third.

Sayo looked at it.

Then nodded.

Ren raised both hands in victory.

Toma laughed.

“Marriage will be interesting.”

Mio glanced at him.

“You’re thinking about marriage?”

“No.”

“Good.”

“Why?”

“You’re still terrible at remembering anniversaries.”

“I haven’t forgotten one.”

“We’ve only had one.”

“Exactly. Perfect record.”

She shook her head.

They continued walking.

That evening, Toma visited his father’s apartment.

Kaito was sitting at his desk, working on a building plan.

He looked up.

“You’re early.”

“Had nothing better to do.”

“Your mother called.”

“About?”

“She wants you both for dinner on Sunday.”

Toma put his bag down.

“Okay.”

Kaito looked at his work again.

A quiet moment passed.

Then he spoke.

“How’s Mio?”

Toma looked at him.

“Why?”

“Just asking.”

“She’s fine.”

“Good.”

Toma leaned against the desk.

“She’s annoying.”

Kaito smiled.

“Your mother used to say the same thing about me.”

“That explains a lot.”

They laughed.

It wasn’t a perfect relationship.

There were still uncomfortable silences.

Questions neither knew how to ask.

But they were trying.

And for now, that was enough.

11:03 PM

Toma’s phone vibrated.

A message.

Unknown number.

He frowned.

He opened it.

There was no text.

Only a location.

WESTERN DISTRICT — OLD BUS TERMINAL.

He stared at it.

Then another message arrived.

You found the wrong man.

Toma slowly sat down.

A third message.

Ask your father what happened to the seventh name.

He looked towards Kaito.

His father was still working.

Toma turned the phone towards him.

Kaito read the messages.

The colour slowly left his face.

Toma noticed.

“Who is it?”

Kaito didn’t answer.

He stood.

Walked to the window.

And looked outside.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Then he spoke.

“There’s something I never told you.”

Toma stared.

“What?”

Kaito turned around.

“Northbridge wasn’t responsible for everything.”

Silence.

“Six people were officially listed in the original investigation.”

He looked at the phone.

“But there were seven.”

Toma felt the familiar weight of an unfinished question.

“Who was the seventh?”

Kaito looked at him.

And for the first time in a year...

He seemed afraid.

“Me.”

[THE END]

Or perhaps... the beginning of another case.
