THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 8 — A Man Who Died Twice

November 2 — 6:30 AM

The place where the city ended was an old coastal road.

There were no buildings nearby.

Only a small bus stop, a closed petrol station and the sea beyond the highway.

Toma arrived with Mio.

Shun stayed behind with Natsumi and Yuri.

They waited.

Ten minutes.

Twenty.

Nothing.

Toma kicked a small stone.

“Maybe this is another terrible idea.”

Mio checked her watch.

“Probably.”

He looked at her.

“You’re supposed to disagree.”

“Why?”

“Morale.”

“Your morale is terrible.”

A car approached.

An old silver sedan.

It stopped near the bus shelter.

The driver’s door opened.

A man stepped out.

Dark hair.

Grey around the temples.

A little thinner than Toma remembered.

A small scar above the left eyebrow.

Toma stopped.

The man looked at him.

For several seconds, neither spoke.

Then—

“Hello, Toma.”

The world seemed to become strangely quiet.

Toma’s hands clenched.

“You’re dead.”

Kaito nodded.

“That’s what everyone believes.”

Toma laughed.

A short, unpleasant sound.

“Six years.”

Kaito didn’t answer.

“Six years, Dad.”

“I know.”

“You let us bury an empty grave?”

Kaito looked down.

Toma stepped closer.

“You let Mom cry for six years?”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

His voice rose.

“Do you actually know what it was like?”

Kaito remained silent.

Toma looked away.

He didn’t want to cry.

He didn’t want to shout.

He didn’t know what he wanted.

“Why?”

Kaito finally answered.

“Because I was afraid.”

“Of what?”

“Of losing you.”

Toma turned back.

“You already lost me.”

Kaito lowered his head.

For the first time, he looked old.

Not physically.

Just tired.

They sat at the bus shelter.

Kaito explained.

Six years ago, he had discovered that Northbridge was falsifying construction inspections. He had collected documents showing that the company had knowingly approved unsafe changes.

Masaki Kuroda helped him.

Yuri helped move the records.

When the company discovered what they were doing, someone arranged an accident.

Kaito survived.

But the body found in the damaged vehicle was never properly identified.

Ueda altered the final report.

The company declared Kaito dead.

And Kaito allowed it.

“Why didn’t you come home?”

“Because I thought they’d use you against me.”

“You could’ve sent a letter.”

“I should have.”

“You could’ve called.”

“I should have.”

“You could’ve told Mom.”

Kaito didn’t reply.

Toma stared at the road.

“You’re a terrible father.”

Kaito nodded.

“Sometimes.”

That answer hurt more than an excuse.

Mio approached.

“Mr Kisaragi, what happened to the four people claiming they met you?”

Kaito looked at her.

“They’re lying.”

“All four?”

“Yes.”

“Why use your identity?”

“Because a dead man is convenient.”

He explained that Genda had arranged for several people to use Kaito’s name when explaining their whereabouts. Each person was given a different story.

The conflicting sightings confused the timeline of the four crimes.

They also made the police look ridiculous.

The entire scheme was designed to bury the original evidence beneath a pile of contradictory statements.

“Where’s the real evidence?” Toma asked.

Kaito looked towards the sea.

“Inside the eastern archive.”

“What’s there?”

“The original inspection records. The payment ledger. And the names of everyone involved.”

“Why not just give it to the police?”

“Because Ueda is one of the names.”

Silence.

Kaito looked at Toma.

“That’s why I called you.”

Toma looked away.

“Don’t mistake this for forgiveness.”

“I won’t.”

“Good.”

Kaito nodded.

Then said something quietly.

“Your mother deserves to know I’m alive.”

Toma looked at him.

“She does.”

He paused.

“But you don’t get to decide when she forgives you.”

Kaito didn’t argue.

Before leaving, Kaito handed Toma a key.

“November 3. Eastern archive. The demolition begins at noon.”

Toma looked at it.

“What happens if we don’t get there?”

Kaito stared towards the terminal in the distance.

“Then six years of evidence disappears.”

He turned towards his son.

“And this time, I won’t have anywhere left to run.”
