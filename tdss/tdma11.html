THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 11 — A Better Kind of Tomorrow

May 18 — Five Months Later

The university courtyard was crowded.

Students were preparing for the annual cultural festival.

Shun was arguing with a food-stall owner.

Mio was trying to finish an article.

Toma was sleeping on a bench.

A fairly ordinary afternoon.

“Move.”

Toma opened one eye.

Mio stood above him.

“Good morning.”

“It’s two in the afternoon.”

“Then good afternoon.”

She sat beside him.

He closed his eyes again.

“You’re getting better at being annoying.”

“Thank you. I’ve been practising.”

She placed a paper bag on his lap.

He opened it.

Two sandwiches.

“You bought food?”

“Don’t sound surprised.”

“You’re usually stealing mine.”

“That’s different.”

Toma laughed.

Their relationship had changed slowly.

There hadn’t been a dramatic confession.

No grand speech.

No sudden realisation beneath the stars.

They had simply started meeting more often.

Coffee after lectures.

Walks through the shopping district.

Arguments about ridiculous things.

Sometimes Mio called him just to complain about an assignment.

Sometimes Toma called her because he had found a new restaurant.

They had become part of each other’s routine.

Neither had officially explained what they were.

Shun found this extremely entertaining.

He had started referring to Mio as Toma’s “unpaid emotional support journalist.”

Mio hated it.

Toma found it hilarious.

That afternoon, they received an invitation.

A small photography exhibition.

The invitation came from Ren and Sayo.

The exhibition was being held at a community art centre.

Its theme was Ordinary Things Worth Keeping.

Toma nearly didn’t go.

But Mio dragged him.

The exhibition was beautiful.

Not extravagant.

Just a series of ordinary moments.

A child laughing.

An old man feeding pigeons.

A crowded train platform.

A cup of coffee abandoned on a windowsill.

Ren stood near the entrance, explaining something to a visitor.

Sayo was arranging small name cards.

When they noticed Toma, Ren waved.

“Hey!”

Toma approached.

“Congratulations.”

“Thanks.”

Sayo smiled.

“Where’s your girlfriend?”

Toma nearly choked.

Mio turned towards him.

“...Girlfriend?”

Ren looked at Sayo.

Sayo looked at Ren.

Then both smiled.

“Interesting,” Ren said.

“Very interesting,” Sayo added.

Toma looked at Mio.

She was suddenly very interested in the exhibition wall.

“She’s not—”

“Don’t finish that sentence,” Mio said.

Ren laughed.

“Come on. I’ll show you something.”

He led them towards a large photograph near the back.

It was a photograph of the sky.

Grey clouds.

A narrow opening of sunlight.

The caption read:

7:03 AM — THE DAY EVERYTHING CHANGED.

Toma looked at it.

“You still take pictures of the sky?”

Ren smiled.

“Always.”

Sayo appeared beside him.

“He says he’s going to take fewer photographs of me.”

“That was one time.”

“You took eleven pictures while I was eating.”

“The lighting was good.”

“Ren.”

“Okay.”

Toma laughed.

For a moment, he remembered the first time he’d heard their names.

A photographer who only photographed the sky.

A girl who had once been afraid to look up.

They seemed happy.

Not perfect.

Just happy.

And somehow, that was nicer.

Later, Mio and Toma stepped outside.

The evening sky was turning orange.

She stood beside him.

“Do you think your father is doing okay?”

Toma nodded.

“He’s writing again.”

“Writing?”

“He started drawing buildings. Apparently, he’s going to work as an architect again someday.”

“That’s good.”

“Maybe.”

Mio looked at him.

“You’re still angry.”

“Sometimes.”

“That’s okay.”

Toma turned towards her.

“You don’t think I should forgive him?”

“That’s not my decision.”

He smiled.

“You’re surprisingly reasonable today.”

“Don’t get used to it.”

They walked towards the station.

After a few minutes, Mio spoke again.

“Toma?”

“Hm?”

“Can I ask you something?”

“Depends.”

“Are we dating?”

He stopped.

She kept walking.

Then realised he wasn’t beside her.

She turned.

“What?”

Toma stared.

“You asked me that?”

“Yes.”

“Just like that?”

“Would you prefer a written application?”

He laughed.

Then walked towards her.

“Well...”

Mio waited.

He scratched his cheek.

“I was planning to ask you.”

“When?”

“Soon.”

“Define soon.”

“Before graduation?”

She stared.

Toma sighed.

“Fine. Yes.”

“Yes what?”

“Yes, we’re dating.”

She smiled.

“Good.”

Then continued walking.

Toma stood there for a second.

“Wait.”

She turned.

“What?”

“You can’t just walk away after that.”

“Why not?”

“Because I haven’t finished being embarrassed.”

Mio laughed.

And for the first time, he didn’t mind being the reason.

That night, Toma returned home.

An envelope was waiting on his desk.

His father’s handwriting.

Inside was a short letter.

Dear Toma,

I don’t expect you to forgive me.

I don’t expect things to return to how they were.

But I wanted to tell you something.

I used to believe that protecting someone meant keeping them away from danger.

I was wrong.

Sometimes it only means letting them stand beside you.

Thank you for finding the truth.

And thank you for giving me a chance to speak.

Dad.

Toma folded the letter.

He put it inside the drawer.

Not because he had forgiven everything.

Not because the past no longer mattered.

But because he didn’t want to throw it away.

Some things deserved time.
