THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 7 — The Names Nobody Remembered

Natsumi refused to go back to the apartment.

She spent the entire evening searching through old documents.

At midnight, she found something.

An old school record.

Her mother’s name was written differently from the name she remembered.

YURI KURODA.

The photograph from six years ago showed her mother using another surname.

YURI SATO.

Natsumi stared at the screen.

“She changed her name.”

Mio sat beside her.

“People do that when they want to disappear.”

Natsumi shook her head.

“Why would she leave me?”

Nobody answered.

Toma stood near the window.

His phone vibrated.

An unknown number.

One message.

Your father made the same mistake. Don’t repeat it.

He showed the others.

Shun looked at the number.

“Someone knows where we are.”

“Wonderful.”

“Stop saying wonderful.”

Toma grabbed his jacket.

“We need to move.”

They spent the next day visiting old addresses connected to Yuri.

Most were empty.

One belonged to a closed restaurant.

Another was a residential building that had been demolished.

Finally, they found a small bakery on the outskirts of the city.

The owner was a woman in her forties.

She stared at Natsumi for a long time.

Then slowly put down the tray she was holding.

“...You’ve grown.”

Natsumi’s eyes filled.

“Mom?”

The woman came around the counter.

Neither spoke for several seconds.

Then Yuri hugged her daughter.

Natsumi didn’t immediately return it.

She stood still.

Then her hands moved.

“Where were you?”

Yuri closed her eyes.

“I’m sorry.”

“Where were you?”

“I was trying to keep you safe.”

“You left me alone!”

Her voice broke.

Toma looked away.

Mio quietly moved towards the door.

Shun followed.

Outside, the street was almost empty.

Toma leaned against the wall.

“That’s difficult.”

Mio stood beside him.

“Yes.”

He looked at her.

“Do you think people always know what they’re doing?”

“No.”

“Then why do they act like they do?”

Mio smiled faintly.

“Because saying ‘I don’t know’ is embarrassing.”

Toma looked at the bakery window.

Inside, Natsumi was sitting opposite her mother.

Neither was smiling.

But they were talking.

“You’re surprisingly good at this,” he said.

“At what?”

“Not making everything worse.”

Mio turned towards him.

“That’s a very strange compliment.”

“It’s the best I’ve got.”

She laughed.

For a moment, things felt almost normal.

Then the bakery door opened.

Yuri stepped outside.

She looked at Toma.

“You must be Kaito’s son.”

He nodded.

Yuri took a folded paper from her pocket.

“Your father gave me this six years ago.”

Toma opened it.

A location.

A time.

And one sentence.

If the terminal is threatened, meet me where the city ends.

Yuri looked towards the road.

“Your father is alive.”
