THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 1 — The Man Who Wasn’t There

October 30 — 8:16 AM

“Your father is dead, correct?”

Toma Kisaragi stared at the detective.

Then at the vending-machine coffee in his hand.

Then back at the detective.

“...That’s a weird way to start a conversation.”

“Answer the question.”

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“Six years.”

“You’re certain?”

Toma placed his coffee on the table.

“There’s a grave with his name on it. We held a funeral. My mother spent two years visiting that grave every Sunday. I think we’re reasonably certain.”

The detective wrote something.

His name was Reiji Nakamura. Late forties, neatly combed hair, rectangular glasses. He had the appearance of someone who hadn’t enjoyed a weekend in several years.

He turned a page in his folder.

“Your father’s name was Kaito Kisaragi?”

“Yes.”

“Occupation?”

“Architect.”

“Any criminal associations?”

Toma blinked.

“Excuse me?”

“Did your father have connections to organised crime?”

“No.”

“Debts? Gambling? Financial disputes?”

“Not that I know of.”

Nakamura closed the folder.

“Interesting.”

Toma leaned back.

“Could someone please tell me what’s going on?”

The detective slid a photograph across the table.

It showed a man entering a convenience store.

Middle-aged. Dark coat. Slightly bent shoulders.

Toma knew that face.

Not because it looked vaguely familiar.

Because it was his father.

The little scar above the left eyebrow. The slightly uneven hairline. Even the way he held his shopping bag.

Toma stopped moving.

“Where did you get this?”

“Security footage. October 17.”

“That’s impossible.”

“Why?”

“Because he’s dead.”

“That’s what we’re trying to establish.”

Toma pushed the photograph away.

“Look, I don’t know what you’re trying to do, but that’s my father. Or someone who looks exactly like him.”

Nakamura opened another file.

“This photograph was submitted as evidence during a financial theft investigation last week. The suspect claims he was having dinner with Kaito Kisaragi on the night the money disappeared.”

“You’re kidding.”

“I don’t joke at work.”

“Sounds miserable.”

Nakamura ignored him.

“Three months ago, another suspect made a similar statement. Two weeks ago, a witness in an assault investigation gave us the same name.”

Toma looked at the documents.

Three separate cases.

Three different people.

All claiming to have met his father.

A man who had been officially dead for six years.

“One more thing,” Nakamura continued.

“What?”

“The fourth case happened yesterday. Someone reported receiving a phone call from your father’s old number.”

Toma’s expression changed.

That number had been disconnected six years ago.

He remembered trying to call it every night after the funeral.

“Who received the call?”

“A woman. She’s missing.”

“Name?”

“Natsumi Kuroda.”

Toma stood.

“I’m done.”

“Toma—”

“I don’t know what that man is doing in your investigation. I don’t know why people are using my father’s name. And I definitely don’t know why you’ve brought me here.”

He picked up his jacket.

Nakamura didn’t stop him.

But just before Toma reached the door, the detective spoke.

“Your father wasn’t the only person declared dead around that time.”

Toma paused.

“Excuse me?”

Nakamura closed the folder.

“Think about that.”

9:42 AM — Outside the Police Station

“You’re seriously telling me they interrogated you before breakfast?”

Toma looked at his phone.

“Technically, it’s brunch.”

“That’s not how time works.”

His closest friend, Shun Arata, was sitting on the low stone wall outside the station, eating a convenience-store rice ball.

Shun was twenty-two, studying at the police academy, and somehow managed to look permanently underdressed.

His shirt was untucked.

One shoelace was undone.

There was mayonnaise on his sleeve.

Toma pointed at it.

“What’s that?”

“Breakfast.”

“Looks like evidence.”

“Don’t start.”

Toma handed him the photograph.

Shun examined it.

His expression gradually changed.

“That’s...”

“Yeah.”

“Your dad?”

“Apparently.”

Shun looked at the photograph again.

“Have you told your mother?”

“No.”

“Good.”

“Why good?”

“Imagine receiving a phone call saying your dead husband is apparently going around town eating dinner with criminals.”

“That’s a horrible way to phrase it.”

“Still accurate.”

Shun returned the photograph.

“Look, I don’t know what’s happening. But don’t investigate this yourself.”

Toma raised an eyebrow.

“Since when did you become sensible?”

“Since I started studying criminal law.”

“Tragic.”

Shun stood up.

“If someone is deliberately using your father’s identity, there’s probably a reason.”

Toma watched him walk away.

He looked down at the photograph again.

Then at his phone.

No missed calls.

No messages.

Nothing.

11:08 AM — Café Lumière

Toma ordered an iced coffee.

It was too cold.

He drank it anyway.

A woman sat opposite him.

Dark cardigan. Canvas shoulder bag. Slightly irritated expression.

“You’re Toma Kisaragi, right?”

“Depends who’s asking.”

“Mio Kanzaki.”

“That doesn’t answer my question.”

“I’m a journalism student.”

“That makes it worse.”

She ignored him.

“I’d like to ask you a few questions about Kaito Kisaragi.”

Toma slowly put his coffee down.

“Are you following me?”

“No.”

“You knew where I was.”

“Your friend posted a picture of this café on his public account.”

Toma opened his phone.

Shun had uploaded a photograph of his rice ball.

Caption: Academy life.

Toma closed his eyes.

“I’m going to kill him.”

“Could you answer my question first?”

“No.”

“I’ll pay for your coffee.”

Toma looked at the glass.

Then at her.

“...Ask one.”

Mio opened her notebook.

“Did your father have any connection to a company called Northbridge Logistics?”

Toma frowned.

“Never heard of it.”

“Are you sure?”

“Pretty sure.”

She wrote something.

“Your father worked as an architect, correct?”

“Yes.”

“Did he ever work on government projects?”

“I was fifteen when he died. I wasn’t checking his employment history.”

“Fair enough.”

She closed her notebook.

“Can I tell you something?”

“You already are.”

“Four separate criminal cases. Four separate suspects. All of them have mentioned the same person.”

“You’re talking about my father.”

“Yes.”

“Then perhaps you should interview the police.”

“I tried.”

“And?”

“They told me to stop asking questions.”

Toma almost laughed.

“That’s usually a sign that you should stop asking questions.”

“Or that I’m getting somewhere.”

She stood.

“Your father wasn’t supposed to be at that convenience store.”

“Meaning?”

“According to the security footage, he entered at 9:14 PM. According to the police, the person whose face appears in that footage was attending a private meeting across town at exactly 9:14 PM.”

Toma stared.

“Who?”

“That’s what I want to find out.”

She walked towards the exit.

Then stopped.

“Oh, and Toma?”

He looked up.

“Your coffee’s getting watery.”

She left.

Toma stared at the melting ice.

“...I hate journalists.”

7:03 PM — Kisaragi Residence

Toma sat on the edge of his bed.

He’d spent the last hour searching through old family documents.

He found nothing useful.

Except an old telephone bill.

His father’s mobile number.

He stared at it.

Then dialled.

He knew it wouldn’t work.

Still, he dialled.

The automated voice began.

“The number you are trying to reach is—”

A click.

Silence.

Then a voice.

“Hello?”

Toma stopped moving.

“Who is this?”

Silence.

Then—

“Toma?”

His knees weakened.

“Dad?”

The voice became quieter.

“Listen carefully. I don’t have much time.”

Toma gripped the phone.

“Where are you? What happened? You’re supposed to be—”

“Don’t trust the police.”

“What?”

“And whatever you do...”

A faint sound came from the other end.

Like someone opening a door.

“Don’t let them find the girl.”

“What girl?”

The call disconnected.

Toma stood motionless.

Call duration: 00:11.

Outside, a vehicle stopped in front of the house.

Its headlights illuminated his bedroom window.

Someone stepped out.

And rang the doorbell.
