THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 4 — The Girl Who Knew Too Much

They didn’t speak on the way back.

Toma sat in the passenger seat while Shun drove.

His phone kept vibrating.

His mother.

Three missed calls.

He ignored them.

Eventually, Shun broke the silence.

“Do you believe him?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Toma looked at him.

“Why good?”

“Because believing everything an old man says is generally a terrible investigative strategy.”

“You’re becoming annoying.”

“Thank you.”

Toma unfolded the envelope again.

Inside was a handwritten note.

If you’re reading this, the company has started moving things again.

Don’t trust any report that carries my signature after September 14.

Find the original inspection record.

And find Masaki.

There was no signature.

Only a small letter.

K.

Mio was waiting at Shun’s apartment.

She had ordered food.

Nobody had eaten it.

“You’re late,” she said.

Toma placed the envelope on the table.

“We met Ono.”

Mio looked at the documents.

Then stopped.

“What happened?”

“Apparently my father didn’t die.”

She didn’t look surprised.

Toma noticed.

“You’re not shocked.”

Mio closed the folder.

“Because I never believed he did.”

Toma stared.

“How long have you known?”

“Six years.”

The room became quiet.

Shun slowly put down his phone.

Toma leaned forward.

“Explain.”

Mio took a deep breath.

“When I was fourteen, my mother worked as an administrative clerk for a company contracted by Northbridge. She handled building inspection paperwork.”

She paused.

“One evening, she came home terrified. She said someone had changed the construction reports. My father wasn’t involved, but my mother had copies.”

“Where are they?”

“She gave them to Kaito.”

Toma looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because he was the only person who believed her.”

Mio continued.

“Two days later, a warehouse connected to the project caught fire. My mother lost her job. The company told the police that the missing documents had been destroyed.”

“Your mother?”

“She left the city with me.”

“Is she alive?”

“Yes.”

Mio looked down.

“We haven’t spoken much since.”

For the first time, Toma noticed that her stubbornness wasn’t confidence.

It was something she had learned to wear.

“Why journalism?” he asked.

She shrugged.

“Because I got tired of people deciding what happened before asking anyone who was there.”

Toma didn’t reply.

Then Mio looked at him.

“I should’ve told you earlier.”

“Yes.”

“I’m sorry.”

He nodded.

It wasn’t forgiveness.

But it was a start.

That evening, they examined Natsumi’s key.

Shun searched for the number.

317.

A locker at the central railway station.

The station had an old storage section scheduled to be renovated.

The locker was still registered.

Toma picked up his jacket.

“Let’s go.”

Mio stood.

“Now?”

“Unless you’d prefer to wait until someone else gets there first.”

Shun grabbed his keys.

“Fine. But I’m driving.”

“Why?”

“Because I don’t trust either of you with a vehicle.”

Natsumi remained seated.

She looked at the metal key.

“There’s something I haven’t told you.”

Everyone turned.

“My father said the locker wasn’t for the documents.”

Toma frowned.

“Then what is it for?”

Natsumi swallowed.

“He said it was for the truth.”
