THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 2 — The Girl at the Door

Toma didn’t immediately open the door.

He looked through the peephole.

Mio.

Of course.

He considered pretending he wasn’t home.

The doorbell rang again.

He opened it.

“Do journalists normally visit people this late?”

“Do you normally ignore someone who’s trying to warn you?”

“Depends on the person.”

Mio stepped inside.

She looked behind herself before closing the door.

“Are you alone?”

“Unfortunately.”

“Anyone else living here?”

“My mother. She’s away.”

Mio nodded.

Then she placed a small black backpack on the floor.

“Someone’s following me.”

Toma looked at the bag.

Then her.

“You brought your problems to my house?”

“I didn’t know where else to go.”

“That’s not exactly reassuring.”

She ignored him and pulled out her phone.

“Did you receive a call?”

Toma went quiet.

“What call?”

“From your father’s number.”

He stared at her.

Mio slowly lowered her phone.

“So you did.”

“How do you know?”

“Because I received the same call.”

She played a recording.

Eleven seconds.

The same voice.

The same words.

Except this time, Toma noticed something.

Just before the call ended, there was a faint sound.

Three knocks.

Then a door opening.

“Where did you get that?”

“Yesterday.”

“Who sent it?”

“I don’t know.”

Toma walked towards the window.

The car was still outside.

Its headlights were off now.

“There’s someone outside.”

Mio glanced towards the curtains.

“That’s why I came.”

“Wonderful. We’re both being stalked.”

“Could you stop being sarcastic for five minutes?”

“I’ve been sarcastic for twenty-one years. It would be difficult.”

She almost smiled.

Then the doorbell rang.

Both froze.

Toma approached the door.

“Who is it?”

A young woman’s voice answered.

“Please. Open the door.”

Mio’s expression changed.

She rushed forward.

“Don’t.”

Toma looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because you don’t know who’s outside.”

The voice came again.

“Mr Kisaragi? My name is Natsumi.”

Mio closed her eyes.

“That’s her.”

Toma opened the door.

A girl stood outside.

Nineteen, perhaps.

Short dark hair. Oversized jacket. A small cut across her lower lip.

She looked exhausted.

Not sleepy.

Exhausted.

She stepped inside and immediately locked the door.

“Are you Kaito’s son?”

Toma stared at her.

“Apparently everyone knows my father except me.”

She swallowed.

“My name is Natsumi Kuroda.”

The missing woman.

Toma looked at Mio.

“You brought a missing person to my house?”

“She was going to be found.”

“By who?”

Natsumi answered.

“People who work for my father’s company.”

They sat in the living room.

Toma brought water.

Nobody drank it.

Natsumi placed a folded document on the table.

“My father worked as an accountant at Northbridge Logistics.”

Toma opened it.

A company employee record.

MASAKI KURODA.

“He’s missing,” she continued. “Three months ago, he disappeared. The police told me he probably ran away because of debt.”

“Did he?”

“No.”

“How do you know?”

“Because he left his shoes at home.”

Toma looked up.

She looked completely serious.

“He never leaves without them. He hates walking barefoot, even indoors.”

For some reason, that answer felt more convincing than a hundred dramatic explanations.

“What does this have to do with my father?”

Natsumi looked at Mio.

Mio nodded.

“Six years ago, my father and Kaito Kisaragi discovered that Northbridge had falsified construction records.”

Toma stopped turning the pages.

“Which construction records?”

“The Northbridge Terminal redevelopment.”

Mio pointed to a section of the document.

“The building was supposed to use a specific grade of structural steel. The company substituted cheaper materials and altered the inspection reports.”

“Was someone hurt?”

“Six workers were injured. The company blamed an electrical failure and paid settlements.”

“And my father?”

“He was the architect who refused to sign the final approval.”

Natsumi looked at him.

“He was supposed to testify.”

Toma stared at the document.

“But he died.”

Mio said nothing.

Natsumi opened her backpack.

Inside was a small metal key.

“My father gave me this before he disappeared.”

“What does it open?”

“I don’t know.”

She turned it over.

A number was engraved on the back.

317.

“There’s something else,” she said.

“My father told me that if anything happened to him, I should find Kaito Kisaragi’s son.”

Toma slowly leaned back.

“Why?”

“Because your father promised him something.”

“What?”

Natsumi looked down.

“That he’d come back.”

Silence.

Then Shun’s voice came from the entrance.

“Does anyone want to explain why there are three people in this house and nobody answered my calls?”

Toma looked towards the doorway.

Shun stood there holding two convenience-store bags.

He surveyed the room.

Then the girl.

Then Mio.

Then Toma.

“...Am I interrupting something?”

“Yes,” Toma replied.

Shun sighed.

“I knew I should’ve stayed home.”

That night, they moved to Shun’s apartment.

He had a spare room.

Barely.

There was a broken chair, three empty boxes and a treadmill that had apparently become a clothes rack.

Natsumi sat quietly on the sofa.

Mio was checking the window locks.

Toma stood in the kitchen.

Shun approached him.

“You’re in trouble.”

“I noticed.”

“Not normal trouble.”

“Thank you for clarifying.”

Shun lowered his voice.

“That girl is officially missing. The police have been searching for her. And you’re now hiding her.”

“She’s not a criminal.”

“Neither am I. Yet.”

Toma looked at him.

“You’re enjoying this.”

“Not even slightly.”

Shun paused.

“One more thing.”

“What?”

“I checked the company.”

He showed Toma his phone.

Northbridge Logistics had announced the sale and demolition of the old terminal.

Demolition scheduled: November 3.

Four days away.

Toma stared at the date.

His father had been declared dead six years ago.

Yet someone was using his name to protect criminals.

And now a missing girl had appeared in his living room with a key to something hidden.

He looked towards Natsumi.

“What’s inside locker 317?”

She shook her head.

“I don’t know.”

Mio closed her notebook.

“Then that’s where we’re going.”
