THE DEAD MAN’S ALIBI

Chapter 5 — People We Meet Along the Way

November 1 — 2:17 PM

The railway station was crowded.

Schoolchildren ran between platforms.

Office workers hurried towards the ticket gates.

Somewhere nearby, a street musician was playing a song badly enough to make Toma reconsider his musical tastes.

They were supposed to meet Natsumi at the west entrance.

She was late.

“She’s three minutes late,” Toma said.

Mio checked her phone.

“People can be late.”

“Three minutes?”

“Yes.”

“Unacceptable.”

Mio looked at him.

“Do you complain this much when you’re alone?”

“More.”

She sighed.

Shun appeared behind them with three drinks.

“Here.”

Toma accepted one.

“Where’s mine?”

“You’re holding it.”

“That’s Mio’s.”

Shun looked down.

“...Oh.”

Mio took the drink from Toma.

“Thanks.”

Shun stared.

“You’re welcome?”

A voice came from behind.

“You’re supposed to ask before stealing someone’s drink.”

Toma turned.

A familiar couple stood near the vending machines.

Ren and Sayo.

Ren wore a camera around his neck.

Sayo held a paper bag from a bakery.

“Shinohara?” Ren called.

Toma recognised them from the neighbourhood.

“Hello.”

Sayo looked at the group.

“Are you all travelling somewhere?”

“No,” Toma replied.

“We’re investigating something,” Shun added.

Toma turned towards him.

“Why would you tell them that?”

“Because it’s obvious.”

Ren looked at Toma.

“You look like you haven’t slept.”

“That’s because I haven’t.”

Sayo handed him the paper bag.

“Here.”

Toma looked inside.

A pastry.

“Why?”

“Because you look like someone who forgot lunch.”

“That’s oddly accurate.”

Mio smiled.

Ren noticed the documents in Toma’s hand.

“Work?”

“Something like that.”

He didn’t ask further.

Instead, he turned towards Sayo.

“Didn’t we come here for the film?”

Sayo stared at him.

“Yes.”

“You have it?”

“No.”

Ren checked his bag.

Then looked at her.

“You’re joking.”

“You were supposed to bring it.”

“I thought you were bringing it.”

They stared at each other.

Toma watched.

Shun whispered, “They’re worse than us.”

Mio nodded.

“Definitely.”

Sayo finally laughed.

“I’ll go buy another one.”

Ren protested.

“That’s wasteful.”

“You forgot it!”

They walked away, still arguing.

Toma looked at the pastry.

Then at Mio.

“Apparently, adults are just children with money.”

“You’re not exactly one to talk.”

“Fair.”

Natsumi arrived five minutes later.

They entered the old storage section.

Locker 317 was near the far end.

The metal key turned with difficulty.

Inside was a small wooden box.

No documents.

No money.

Just an envelope and an old cassette recorder.

Toma opened the envelope.

There was a handwritten note.

For the person who finds this.

Northbridge didn’t destroy the records.

They moved them.

The original inspection documents are inside the terminal’s eastern archive.

The key is not important.

The person who knows where to look is.

Natsumi read it twice.

“My father wrote this.”

Mio examined the cassette recorder.

“There’s a tape.”

Toma pressed play.

Static.

Then a man’s voice.

“To whoever is listening...”

Toma recognised it.

His father’s.

“If this recording reached you, the original reports are probably in danger.”

A pause.

“Masaki, if you’re listening, take the girl and leave the city.”

The recording continued.

“To my son...”

Toma stopped breathing.

“I don’t know whether you’ll ever hear this. I’m sorry.”

His father’s voice faltered.

“I wanted to explain everything. I really did.”

A pause.

“But some things are easier to leave unfinished.”

The recording ended.

Nobody spoke.

Then a second sound came from the recorder.

A faint mechanical click.

The tape continued.

Another voice.

Not his father.

A younger man.

“Mr Kisaragi?”

Static.

“Your son is going to find out.”

The recording stopped.

Shun rewound it.

There was nothing else.

Toma looked at the cassette.

“Who was that?”

Natsumi answered quietly.

“My father.”

She turned the recorder over.

On its underside was a small handwritten label.

NOVEMBER 3 — EASTERN ARCHIVE.

The same date as the terminal demolition.

Toma looked at the others.

“We have two days.”
